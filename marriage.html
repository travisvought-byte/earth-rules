Getting married.

Marriage is a legal contract as well as a promise, so there are a few papers to get right.

Love and familyTopic 3 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: A legal marriage is a union recorded by the government, which starts with a marriage license and a ceremony.

Why it matters: Being married changes your taxes, benefits and family rights, so it helps to know the steps and what changes.

Do this first

  1. Both of you go to the county probate court with photo ID and Social Security numbers.
  2. Bring a certified copy of any divorce decree or death certificate from a past marriage.
  3. Have your ceremony within 60 days, with a judge, mayor or registered minister.
  4. Get certified copies of your marriage record afterward.
  5. Change your name with Social Security first, then at the BMV.
  6. Update your tax withholding and tell your employer about any name change.

Changing your name

  1. Social Security first. Apply for a corrected Social Security card. You need proof of identity, your new name and the marriage. Social Security wants original or certified documents, not photocopies. Start at ssa.gov (opens in a new tab).
  2. Then the BMV. In Ohio, bring the original or a certified copy of your marriage certificate or license to a deputy registrar for an updated license or ID.
  3. Then everyone else. Your employer, bank, passport, voter registration and doctors.

Your tax return must match Social Security's records, or your refund can be delayed. See papers and ID.

What marriage changes

  • Taxes. If you are married on December 31, you count as married for the whole year. You can file jointly or separately. The IRS suggests checking both and using the one with the lower tax. Update your W-4 at work. See taxes.
  • Social Security. If your spouse dies, you may get survivor benefits. Usually you must have been married at least 9 months, with some exceptions.
  • Health coverage. If you get help paying for a Marketplace plan, report the marriage. See coverage.
  • Family and property. Marriage can change who inherits and who speaks for you if you cannot. A will makes your wishes clear. See when someone dies.

Everywhere

UNICEF calls marriage before age 18 a fundamental violation of human rights. That includes informal unions where a child lives with a partner as if married. Both people should be adults who freely choose to marry.

In the United States

Each state sets its own marriage rules, and licenses come from a local court or office. Since the Supreme Court decided Obergefell v. Hodges on June 26, 2015, every state must allow same-sex couples to marry and must recognize those marriages from other places. The case came from Ohio.

In Ohio

Getting married in Ohio
TopicThe rule
Minimum age18
Age 17Only with juvenile court consent, marriage counseling, and a partner no more than 4 years older
WhereProbate court in the county where either of you lives
License lasts60 days

Both of you must appear in person. Bring photo ID, your Social Security numbers, and certified papers for any past marriage that ended. Fees are set by each county, so check your county probate court's website. Franklin County charged $65 in 2026. A 17-year-old cannot get a license until 14 days after the juvenile court consents. Ohio's written law still says marriage is between a man and a woman, but the Supreme Court ruling overrides it.
