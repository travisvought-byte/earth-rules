EARTH RULES · HELP NOW

Losing your housing.

If you're about to lose your home or have nowhere to sleep tonight, there are people whose whole job is to help you.

Help nowTopic 2 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: Where to turn if you are at risk of homelessness or already homeless.

Why it matters: Acting fast, before an eviction or the first night outside, opens up more help and keeps more of your options.

Do this first

  1. Call 211 to find shelter, food and rent help near you.
  2. If you got an eviction notice, get legal help right away. Don't just leave.
  3. Veterans, call 1-877-424-3838 any time.
  4. Young people, call or text 1-800-786-2929.
  5. Set up a way to get mail so you don't miss benefits or court papers.

Numbers to call

  • 211Local help: shelter, food, rent
  • 1-877-424-3838Homeless Veterans, 24/7

Tonight

  • Call 211. United Way's 211 connects you with local help for housing costs, utilities and food. Every call is confidential.
  • Search HUD's Find Shelter (opens in a new tab) tool for shelters, food pantries, health clinics and clothing near you.
  • In danger, call 911.

Before you lose your home

If you're behind on rent or got an eviction notice, read tenant rights. Free legal aid can often help. Each area has a Continuum of Care, a local network that helps people at risk of homelessness make a plan and find resources. HUD's Find Shelter page links to yours.

Veterans and young people

  • Veterans: the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans is free, confidential and open 24/7 at 1-877-424-3838. You don't need to be enrolled in VA health care. Family and friends can call for you. See veteran rights.
  • Youth and teens: the National Runaway Safeline is open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Call or text 1-800-786-2929 or chat online. You don't have to give your name. Its Home Free program can give a free bus ticket home or to a safe place.

Mail and ID without an address

The Post Office offers General Delivery for people with no fixed address and no ID. Mail is held at a Post Office for you to pick up, usually for up to 30 days. Ask your local Post Office. Some shelters also let you use their address. For a new ID or birth certificate, see papers. For food and phone help, see basics.

Everywhere

Getting help early, before you lose your home, keeps the most doors open. Keep copies of your ID and important papers somewhere safe.

In the United States

HUD funds local Continuum of Care programs in communities across the country. The VA runs programs for homeless and at-risk veterans. 211 covers most of the country.

In Ohio

The Coalition on Homelessness and Housing in Ohio (COHHIO) runs a Housing Information Line at 1-888-485-7999. It helps with landlord-tenant law, fair housing and affordable housing questions. Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab) has free eviction guides and forms.
