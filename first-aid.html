First aid.

A few simple moves can keep someone alive until help gets there.

BodyTopic 1 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: First aid is the help you give in the first few minutes after someone is hurt or gets sick.

Why it matters: Help can take minutes to arrive, and what you do in those minutes can save a life.

Do this first

  1. Make sure the scene is safe for you before you go in.
  2. Call 911, or point at one person and tell them to call.
  3. If an adult collapses and is not breathing normally, push hard and fast in the center of the chest.
  4. For bad bleeding, press hard on the wound and keep pressing.
  5. For a burn, cool it under clean, cool running water.
  6. For a possible poisoning, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222.

Hands-only CPR for an adult

If a teen or adult suddenly collapses, the American Heart Association says there are two steps: call 911, then push hard and fast in the center of the chest.

  1. Tap their shoulder and ask if they are OK. Look for normal breathing.
  2. Have someone call 911 and bring an AED (a heart shock box) if there is one.
  3. Lay them on their back on a firm, flat surface.
  4. Put the heel of one hand in the center of the chest and your other hand on top.
  5. Keep your arms straight. Push at least 2 inches deep, 100 to 120 times a minute. Let the chest come back up each time.
  6. Use the AED as soon as it arrives. Keep going until help takes over.

Bleeding, burns and choking

  • Bleeding: The Red Cross says to press firmly right on the wound with a clean cloth or dressing, and hold it until the bleeding stops or help arrives. Wear gloves if you have them. Call 911 for bleeding that will not stop.
  • Burns: Cool the burn under clean, cool running water for 5 to 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter or grease. Cover it loosely with a clean, dry, non-stick cloth. A burn that blisters, is large, or is on the face, hands, feet, joints or groin needs a doctor. Call 911 for deep, large, electrical or chemical burns.
  • Choking: If an adult cannot cough, talk or breathe, call 911. Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. Then give 5 quick inward and upward thrusts with your fist just above the belly button. Keep switching until they can cough or talk. If they pass out, start CPR.

Poisoning

Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222, connects you to your local poison center. It is toll-free, open 24/7, and works in more than 100 languages. Call even if the person seems fine. If they are not breathing, will not wake up, or are having a seizure, call 911 first.

Take a class

Reading helps, but practice sticks. The American Red Cross (opens in a new tab) has online and in-person CPR and first aid classes. An online course can take as little as 4 hours. Online-only classes do not count for most job requirements, because those need an in-person skills check. You can also ask your local fire department or hospital whether they hold classes.

Everywhere

The basics are the same everywhere: make sure it is safe, call for help, keep blood in the body, and keep blood moving with chest pushes if the heart stops. Learn your local emergency number before you travel.

In the United States

In the US, call 911 for any emergency. Poison Help (1-800-222-1222) is a program of the federal Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA), and works across the country.
