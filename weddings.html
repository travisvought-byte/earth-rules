Weddings and customs.

Weddings look very different around the world, but almost all of them celebrate two people joining their lives.

Love and familyTopic 4 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: A wedding is the ceremony where a couple marries, often with family, friends, food and customs that carry meaning.

Why it matters: Knowing the basics helps you plan your own wedding, respect someone else's, and be a good guest.

Do this first

  1. Get your marriage license first. A ceremony alone is not a legal marriage.
  2. Choose a civil ceremony, a religious one, or both.
  3. Set a budget you can afford. A courthouse wedding costs very little.
  4. As a guest, reply by the date on the invitation.
  5. Ask about customs you do not know. People are usually glad to explain.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency, including forced marriage danger

Civil and religious ceremonies

A civil ceremony is done by a government official, like a judge or mayor, often at a courthouse. A religious ceremony is led by a faith leader in that tradition's way. Many couples have one, and some have both. In Ohio, a judge, a mayor, or a minister registered with the Ohio Secretary of State can perform a legal marriage.

Customs from different traditions

Customs vary a lot within every faith and culture. A few well-known examples:

Examples of wedding customs
TraditionOne custom
HinduThe couple circles a sacred fire, seen as a witness, and takes seven steps together.
JewishThe couple stands under a canopy called a huppah, a symbol of their new home. A glass is often broken.
MuslimMarriage is a contract. The groom gives the bride a gift called mahr for her own security.
ChristianThe couple makes vows before a priest or minister, with readings and a blessing.
Many culturesJoining hands, swapping rings, and gifts to help the new couple.

Arranged marriage and forced marriage

In an arranged marriage, families take a leading role in choosing the partner, but both people are free to say yes or no. That is a choice many people make happily.

A forced marriage is when one or both people do not agree and are pressured, tricked or threatened into it. If someone agrees at first and then changes their mind but is still made to marry, that is forced marriage too. The US government calls it a human rights abuse, and child abuse when it involves minors. It is a crime in the UK. If you are in danger, call 911. US citizens abroad can contact the nearest US embassy or consulate. See leaving abuse.

Being a good wedding guest

  • Reply by the date asked, and do not bring extra people unless the invitation names them.
  • Follow any dress code or religious custom, like covering your head or removing shoes, if asked.
  • A gift is a kind gesture, not a test. Give what fits your budget, or a heartfelt card.

Everywhere

Almost every culture marks marriage with a ritual that shows two people are now joined, like joining hands or exchanging rings. Gifts to the couple are part of many weddings. Whatever the custom, both people must be adults who freely agree.

In the United States

US Citizenship and Immigration Services says forced marriage involves force, fraud or coercion and takes away a person's final choice of whether, when and whom to marry. Help is available 24 hours a day. Call 911 in an emergency.

In Ohio

A wedding is legal in Ohio only with a license from a county probate court, used within 60 days. For a simple, low-cost civil ceremony, ask your local municipal court about having a judge marry you. See getting married for the license steps.
