Reading a bill.

Bills have mistakes more often than you'd think, and you're allowed to question every line.

Money and papersTopic 5 of 12

In 10 seconds

What it is: This page shows how to check a medical, utility or phone bill and what to do if it's wrong or too big to pay.

Why it matters: Checking a bill takes a few minutes and can save you real money.

Do this first

  1. Ask for an itemized bill that lists every charge.
  2. For medical bills, compare the bill to your insurance company's Explanation of Benefits.
  3. Call the billing office about any charge you don't recognize.
  4. Ask about a payment plan or financial help before you fall behind.
  5. Never pay a bill from a sender you don't know. Look up the company's number yourself.

Numbers to call

  • 1-800-985-3059No Surprises Help Desk
  • 1-877-742-5622Ohio Consumers' Counsel

Medical bills

After a visit, your insurance company sends an Explanation of Benefits (EOB). An EOB is not a bill. It shows what was charged, what the plan paid and your "patient balance."

  1. Wait for the actual bill from the doctor or hospital.
  2. Ask for an itemized bill.
  3. Check that the services, dates and amounts match the EOB. Your bill should not be more than the patient balance on the EOB.
  4. Look for double charges, which are common when you saw more than one provider.
  5. If something is wrong, call the provider's billing office. Your insurance company can help fix errors too.

If you can't pay

  • First make sure you really owe the amount.
  • Ask the hospital for its financial assistance policy. Hospitals must give it to you free, and it explains who gets free or lower-cost care.
  • Ask for an interest-free payment plan, and get the terms in writing.
  • Ask if they'll lower the bill.
  • Be careful putting a medical bill on a credit card or medical credit card. You may pay high interest and lose the chance to bargain.

Phone and utility bills

Read your phone bill every month. Look for small charges with vague names like "service fee" or "membership" that you didn't sign up for. Call the company that charged you and ask them to remove it. Then call your phone company.

For gas or electric, call the utility first if a bill looks wrong. Ask about payment plans before a shutoff notice.

Fake bills are a common scam. Scammers often say you must pay right away, by gift card, wire transfer or crypto. Hang up and call the number on a past bill. See scams.

Everywhere

In any country, a bill is a claim, not a fact. You can ask what each charge is for, and a business should be able to show you.

In the United States

No Surprises Act. Since 2022, most emergency care is protected from surprise bills, even from an out-of-network hospital. At an in-network hospital, out-of-network doctors like anesthesiologists can't surprise-bill you for certain services. If you have no insurance or don't use it, you can get a good faith estimate before care. If the bill is $400 or more above the estimate, you can dispute it within 120 days of the bill date. Help: 1-800-985-3059 or CMS.gov (opens in a new tab).

In Ohio

Ohio gas and electric utilities regulated by the state must offer payment plans if you fall behind:

  • One-sixth plan: pay the past-due amount in 6 equal monthly payments, plus your current bill.
  • One-ninth plan: pay the past-due amount in 9 equal payments on a budget plan.
  • Winter plan (November 1 to April 15): pay one-third of your total balance each month.
  • PIPP Plus: for lower-income homes, you pay a share of your income. Sign up through your local community action agency.

The Ohio Consumers' Counsel (opens in a new tab) can help: 1-877-742-5622. See basics for heating help.
