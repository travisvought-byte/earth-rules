EARTH RULES · TAXES

Filing your taxes, free.

What you need, where to file without paying, and the dates that matter.

Do I have to file?

It depends on your income, age and filing status, and the limits change every year. If tax was taken out of your pay, file anyway: it's the only way to get it back. The IRS has a free tool that answers the question: Do I need to file? (opens in a new tab).

Ways to file for free

  • IRS Free File (opens in a new tab): brand-name software, free if your income is under the year's limit.
  • VITA and TCE (opens in a new tab): trained volunteers prepare your return for free if you have a low or moderate income, a disability, limited English, or are 60 or older (TCE).
  • IRS Direct File is not being offered for the 2026 filing season.

What to have ready

  • W-2s from each job and any 1099s (side work, interest, unemployment).
  • Your Social Security number, and those of anyone you claim.
  • Bank account and routing numbers for a direct-deposit refund.
  • Last year's return, if you have it.

Deadlines and after

Returns are due April 15. An extension moves the filing date to October 15 but not the payment, so pay what you expect to owe by April 15. Keep your return and papers for at least three years. Most states have their own income tax return too; see your state.

The IRS does not start contact with a text, email or social media message demanding payment. Those are scams.
