Buying a car.

A car costs much more than the monthly payment, so shop for the whole deal.

Money and papersTopic 7 of 12

In 10 seconds

What it is: This page covers buying a car, paying for it, keeping it running and what to do after a crash.

Why it matters: For most people a car is the second biggest thing they ever buy, and a bad loan or a lemon can cost thousands.

Do this first

  1. Get loan pre-approvals from a bank or credit union before you visit a dealer.
  2. Compare the total cost of the loan, not just the monthly payment.
  3. Check for open recalls with the car's 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
  4. Pay an independent mechanic to inspect any used car before you buy.
  5. Check tire pressure monthly using the label on the driver's door edge.

Numbers to call

  • 1-888-327-4236NHTSA vehicle safety
  • 1-800-282-0515Ohio Attorney General consumer help

Paying for it

You don't have to get your loan from the dealer. Get pre-approvals from a bank, credit union or other lender first. Then you can compare, and use the best offer to bargain.

Look at four things: the amount, the APR, the length and the payment. A longer loan means a smaller payment but more interest. In the CFPB's example, a $20,000 loan at 4.75% costs about $1,500 in interest over 3 years and about $3,000 over 6 years. Some experts say keep car loans to 5 years or less. Long loans make it more likely you owe more than the car is worth. See credit.

Buying used

  • Dealers must post a Buyers Guide sticker on every used car. It says if there is a warranty.
  • "As Is" means the dealer won't pay for repairs after the sale.
  • Get any spoken promise written on the Buyers Guide.
  • Get a vehicle history report, then pay an independent mechanic to inspect it. If the dealer says no, go elsewhere.
  • Federal law gives you no 3-day right to return a car. Get any return policy in writing.

Recalls and upkeep

Look up your VIN (on the lower left of the windshield or your registration) at nhtsa.gov/recalls (opens in a new tab). Recall repairs are free at a dealer.

  • Tires: check pressure at least monthly, when the car has sat for 3 hours. Use the number on the label on the driver's door edge or in your owner's manual, not the number on the tire.
  • Tread: put a penny in the tread with Lincoln's head upside down. If you can see the top of his head, replace the tires.
  • Everything else: follow the maintenance schedule in your owner's manual. Have the battery, belts, wipers and lights checked before winter.

After a crash

  1. Check for injuries. Call 911 if anyone is hurt.
  2. Stop and stay at the scene. Share your name and insurance information.
  3. Be polite, but don't admit fault.
  4. Take photos. Get names and phone numbers of witnesses.
  5. Get the police report number. Call your insurance company soon, and keep notes of every call.

Everywhere

Everywhere, the true cost of a car is the price plus interest, insurance, fuel, repairs and parking. A cheaper car with a short loan usually beats a nicer car with a long one.

In the United States

The CFPB (opens in a new tab) explains car loans. The FTC (opens in a new tab) explains the Buyers Guide. NHTSA: 1-888-327-4236.

In Ohio

Lemon law. Ohio's lemon law covers new cars with a warranty problem that seriously hurts their use, value or safety in the first 12 months or 18,000 miles, whichever comes first. The maker has had a fair chance to fix it if:

  • the same problem was repaired 3 or more times, or
  • the car was in the shop 30 or more days total, or
  • there were 8 or more repair attempts, or
  • one repair failed on a problem that could kill or seriously hurt someone.

Then you can choose a new car or a full refund. Start with a certified letter to the maker. The Ohio Attorney General (opens in a new tab) explains the steps. Consumer complaints: 1-800-282-0515.

Crashes. Ohio law says a driver in a crash on a public road must stop and give their name, address and vehicle registration number to anyone hurt, the other drivers and the police. If you hit a parked car, leave that information in writing on it.
