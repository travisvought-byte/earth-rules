Making friends as an adult.

It's a skill, it takes hours, and almost everyone finds it harder after school.

It takes time, and that's normal

About 50 hourstogether to go from acquaintance to casual friend.
About 90 hoursto become friends.
200+ hoursto become close friends.

From a 2018 University of Kansas study of adults who had recently moved and of college freshmen. Time spent hanging out counted more than time spent working side by side.

What works

  • Go to the same thing every week: a class, a league, a congregation, a volunteer shift. Seeing the same faces is how the hours add up.
  • Be the one who asks. Most people are waiting to be invited, and people tend to underestimate how much others enjoy talking with them.
  • Follow up within a few days. "That was fun, want to do it again Thursday?" is enough.
  • Keep the friends you have. A short check-in every few weeks keeps a friendship from fading.
  • Expect some invitations to go nowhere. That's the average, not a verdict on you.

If it's become loneliness

Loneliness is common and it affects health. If it has become hard to get through the day, call or text 988 to talk with someone, any time. Local groups and activities are often listed through 211.
