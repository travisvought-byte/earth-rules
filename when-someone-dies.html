EARTH RULES · WHEN SOMEONE DIES

When someone dies.

What to do first, what comes after, and what to set up now.

The first days

  1. If the death was unexpected at home, call 911. If the person was in hospice, call the hospice nurse.
  2. Call a funeral home. They move the body and usually report the death to Social Security. Under US law they must give you an itemized price list; you only pay for what you choose.
  3. Order death certificates. Get several certified copies. Banks, insurers and agencies each want one.
  4. Look for a will and any written funeral wishes.

The weeks after

  • Social Security: call 800-772-1213. Family members may be owed survivor benefits.
  • Veterans: VA burial benefits (opens in a new tab), including a free burial in a national cemetery and a headstone.
  • Banks, insurers, employer, pension: call each one. Ask about life insurance and final paychecks.
  • Credit bureaus: ask them to flag the credit file as deceased to stop identity theft.
  • Probate: the court process that settles what they owned and owed. In Ohio it's the county probate court, and small estates can use a shorter process.
  • Debts: you usually don't owe a family member's debts unless you co-signed. Debt collectors may not say you do.

Do this now, for the people you love

  • A will: who gets what, and who cares for your children.
  • Power of attorney: who handles your money if you can't.
  • Health care wishes: a living will and a health care power of attorney. Ohio has standard free forms; Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab) explains them.
  • Beneficiaries: name them on bank accounts, retirement accounts and life insurance. These pass outside the will.
  • Put it all in your papers folder and tell someone where it is.

Grief has no schedule. If it's become too heavy, call or text 988.
