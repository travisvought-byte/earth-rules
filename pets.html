Pets.

A pet can be family. Here is how to get one and keep it healthy and safe.

Skills and funTopic 3 of 4

In 10 seconds

What it is: A pet is an animal you take care of every day, for its whole life, like a dog, cat, fish or small animal.

Why it matters: Pets cost real money and time, and a little planning keeps both you and your animal safe and healthy.

Do this first

  1. Adopt from a local shelter or rescue first.
  2. Take a new pet to a vet within a week.
  3. Keep rabies shots and other vaccines up to date.
  4. Get a microchip and register it with your phone number.
  5. In Ohio, buy a dog license from your county auditor every year.
  6. Save the animal poison line in your phone: (888) 426-4435.

Numbers to call

  • 1-888-426-4435ASPCA Animal Poison Control, 24/7 (a fee may apply)

Ways to spend less: spay or neuter your pet (many shelters offer low cost or free surgery), keep up with yearly checkups, prevent fleas and ticks, brush your pet's teeth with pet toothpaste, and do not overfeed.

Foods that can hurt pets

The ASPCA says to keep these people foods away from pets:

  • Chocolate, coffee and caffeine. The darker the chocolate, the more dangerous.
  • Grapes and raisins. They can hurt a dog's kidneys.
  • Onions, garlic and chives. They can damage red blood cells.
  • Xylitol, a sweetener in sugar free gum, candy, baked goods and some toothpaste. It can cause low blood sugar and liver damage.
  • Alcohol, raw bread dough, macadamia nuts, very salty food, and bones.

If your pet eats something bad, note how much, then call your vet or ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435. It is open 24 hours a day. A consultation fee may apply.

Pets in a disaster

  • Put your pets in your family emergency plan. If you are told to leave, take them with you.
  • Many public shelters and hotels do not allow pets. Make a list of pet friendly places ahead of time.
  • Ask a neighbor or friend to care for your pets if you are away when trouble hits.
  • Pack a pet kit: food, water, medicine, vet and vaccine records, a carrier or leash, a spare collar with ID tag, and a recent photo of you with your pet.

See go-bag and home emergencies for your own kit.

Microchips and staying clean

Microchip. A microchip can help you find a lost pet, even after a disaster. It only works if the registration has your current contact information, so update it every time you move or change your phone number. Also put your name and phone number on your pet's ID tag.

Germs. Some germs pass between animals and people. Wash your hands with soap and water after touching pets, their food, bowls, toys or poop, and before you eat. Pick up dog poop, especially where kids play. Help children under 5 wash their hands, and always watch young kids around dogs.

Help paying for pet care

  • Shelter and community clinics often offer low cost shots and spay or neuter.
  • Veterinary schools sometimes run low cost clinics.
  • Ask your vet about a weekly or monthly payment plan.
  • Local shelters and rescues sometimes have funds to help families keep their pets.
  • Call 211 for local help. Some food pantries also give out pet food. See basics.

Everywhere

Keep your pet's rabies shots current, as your local law requires. Rabies shots should be given by a vet. Take your pet for regular checkups, and wash your hands after touching animals. Never share people food with pets unless you know it is safe.

In the United States

Service animals are not pets. Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, a service animal is a dog trained to do a task for a person with a disability. A dog that only gives comfort or emotional support is not a service animal under the ADA. Businesses may ask only two things: is the dog needed because of a disability, and what task is it trained to do. See disability rights.

In Ohio

Dog license. Every dog over 3 months old must be registered with your county auditor. Renew between December 1 and January 31 each year, or buy a 3 year or permanent license. Register a new dog right away. Your dog must wear its tag. Guide, hearing and support dogs can get a free, permanent license. Fees vary by county.

Rabies. Rabies shot rules are set by local law. Ask your vet or your local health district which shots your dog or cat needs where you live.
