Helping someone struggling.

You do not need training to help someone who is hurting. You need to ask, listen and stay.

BodyTopic 5 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: This is how to spot when someone may be in crisis, what to say, and where to get help, for them or for you.

Why it matters: A caring person who asks directly and helps someone connect to support can save a life.

Do this first

  1. Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?"
  2. Listen without judging. Let them talk.
  3. If they are in danger now, do not leave them alone. Call 911.
  4. Help them call or text 988, or call it together.
  5. Keep checking in over the next days and weeks.

Numbers to call

  • 988Suicide and Crisis Lifeline
  • 911Emergency
  • 1-800-662-4357SAMHSA Helpline

Warning signs

The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) lists signs like these. Get help fast if they are new or getting worse.

  • Talking about wanting to die, feeling like a burden, or deep guilt or shame
  • Feeling hopeless, trapped, or in pain that feels unbearable
  • Making a plan, looking up ways to die, or giving away prized things
  • Pulling away from people or saying goodbye
  • Big mood swings, more drinking or drug use, or sleeping and eating much more or less

Asking does not plant the idea

Many people are afraid to ask. NIMH says studies show that asking people about suicidal thoughts does not cause or increase them. Asking can open the door. Then:

  1. Ask. Use the word suicide.
  2. Be there. Listen. You do not have to fix it.
  3. Help keep them safe. Ask if they have a plan. Help put distance between them and anything deadly.
  4. Help them connect to 988 and to people they trust.
  5. Follow up. Staying in touch after a crisis makes a real difference.

If someone tells you they are going to kill themselves, do not leave them alone, and do not promise to keep it secret. Call 911 if their life is in danger right now.

988 and the Veterans Crisis Line

  • 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org (opens in a new tab). It is free and confidential. You can call if you are worried about someone else. For Spanish, press 2 or text AYUDA to 988.
  • Veterans and service members: dial 988 then press 1, or text 838255. You do not have to be enrolled in VA care. Family and friends can call too.

Finding ongoing help

Treatment works. To find a therapist, counselor or treatment program near you:

  • FindTreatment.gov (opens in a new tab) is a free, confidential and anonymous federal search for mental health and substance use care.
  • SAMHSA's National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357, is free, open 24/7, in English and Spanish. It gives referrals, not counseling.
  • Your doctor can also refer you. See coverage for how insurance helps pay, and help for more hotlines.

The same steps work for you. If you are the one struggling, you can call or text 988 too.

Everywhere

Everywhere, asking directly, listening, and connecting someone to a trusted person or professional helps. Many countries have their own crisis lines. See help.

In the United States

988 works anywhere in the US by call, text or chat. SAMHSA, a federal agency, runs the National Helpline and FindTreatment.gov.

In Ohio

Ohio also runs the Ohio CareLine, 1-800-720-9616, a free, 24/7 call line staffed by behavioral health professionals for personal or family crises.
