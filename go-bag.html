Disaster go-bag.

A packed bag and a simple family plan turn a disaster from chaos into a list you already made.

Help nowTopic 4 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: What to pack in an emergency kit and how to make a family plan for storms, fires, floods and power outages.

Why it matters: In a disaster, stores close, power goes out and phones jam, so what you prepared ahead is what you'll have.

Do this first

  1. Store 1 gallon of water per person per day.
  2. Pack food, medicine, a light, a radio and copies of your papers.
  3. Pick two meeting places and one out-of-area contact.
  4. Sign up for your county's emergency alerts.
  5. Practice your plan twice a year, and check your kit.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency

Pack the basics

  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day, for drinking and washing. The Red Cross suggests 3 days' worth to grab and go, 2 weeks' worth at home.
  • Food that doesn't spoil, and a manual can opener
  • Medicine: a 7-day supply, plus glasses and medical items
  • Flashlight, extra batteries, first aid kit and a whistle
  • Battery or hand-crank radio, including NOAA Weather Radio
  • Phone charger and backup battery
  • Cash, local maps, a warm blanket and a change of clothes
  • Copies of papers: IDs, insurance, bank records, medicine list, in a waterproof bag or saved online
  • Baby supplies and pet food and water if you need them

Make a plan

  • Pick two meeting places: one right outside your home, one outside your neighborhood.
  • Choose an out-of-area contact. Long-distance calls and texts often get through when local lines are jammed. Write the number down.
  • Know your evacuation routes, with backups.
  • Plan for pets. Keep a list of pet-friendly hotels and shelters along your route.
  • Practice twice a year, and replace expired food and medicine.

Power outages

  • Run generators outside only, at least 20 feet from doors and windows. Never in a garage.
  • Put battery-backed carbon monoxide alarms on every floor.
  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A fridge keeps food cold about 4 hours. A full freezer stays safe about 48 hours.
  • Unplug electronics to avoid damage from surges.
  • If you rely on medical devices or medicine that needs to stay cold, plan with your doctor ahead of time.

Everywhere

The basics are the same anywhere: water, food, medicine, light, a way to get news, and a plan to find each other.

In the United States

FEMA's Ready.gov (opens in a new tab) has full kit lists and plan forms. The American Red Cross (opens in a new tab) runs disaster shelters and has planning guides. Put key dates on your calendar, like checking your kit.
