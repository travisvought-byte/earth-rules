Attention traps.

Why it's hard to put the phone down, and how to win some time back.

BrainTopic 6 of 7

You're not the customer

Free apps make money from ads, and ads pay for time. So the apps are built to keep you looking as long as possible. That's not a flaw. It's the business.

The traps

  • Endless scroll and autoplay

    No bottom to the page and the next video starts on its own, so there's never a natural place to stop.

    Your move: Decide when you'll stop before you start. Set a timer.

  • Slot-machine rewards

    Pull to refresh, and sometimes there's something good. Rewards that come at random keep people pulling, like a slot machine.

    Your move: Turn off pull-to-refresh habits: check at set times.

  • Outrage

    Posts with angry, moral words spread further. One study found each added moral-emotional word raised sharing by about 20%.

    Your move: If a post makes you furious, wait before you share it.

  • Notifications

    Red dots and buzzes are built to pull you back in.

    Your move: Turn off every notification that isn't from a real person.

  • The algorithm

    The feed shows what kept you watching last time, not what's true or good for you. Over time it can narrow what you see.

    Your move: Follow people on purpose, and use a chronological feed where you can.

Small fixes that work

  • Use the screen time limits built into your phone.
  • Keep the phone out of the bedroom at night. Use a cheap alarm clock.
  • Move the apps that eat your time off the home screen.
