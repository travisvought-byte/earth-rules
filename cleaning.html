Cleaning.

A little cleaning often beats a big cleaning once in a while.

HomeTopic 2 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: Cleaning removes dirt and most germs, and disinfecting kills the germs that are left.

Why it matters: A clean home keeps you healthier, keeps pests away, and stops small messes from turning into mold or damage.

Do this first

  1. Pick one small daily habit, like wiping the kitchen counter after dinner.
  2. Clean with soap or detergent and water first. That removes most germs.
  3. Disinfect only when someone is sick or has a weak immune system.
  4. Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner.
  5. Open a window or run a fan while you clean.

Numbers to call

  • 1-800-222-1222Poison Help
  • 911Emergency

A routine that sticks

Every day

  • Wash dishes or load the dishwasher.
  • Wipe kitchen counters and the table.
  • Put things back where they go for 10 minutes.

Every week

  • Clean the bathroom sink, toilet and tub.
  • Vacuum or sweep floors, then mop the kitchen and bathroom.
  • Wipe high-touch spots: doorknobs, light switches, remotes, phones.
  • Wash sheets and towels. Take out trash and recycling.

Every month

  • Clean out the fridge and wipe the shelves.
  • Dust high places and fan blades.
  • Check under sinks and around tubs for leaks or mold.

Clean vs. disinfect

Cleaning with soap or detergent, water and scrubbing removes most germs. For most homes, most of the time, that is enough.

Disinfecting kills most germs that are left. Do it when someone at home is sick or has a weak immune system, and on anything raw meat or chicken touched.

  • Clean the surface first. Dirt blocks disinfectant.
  • Read the label. Keep the surface wet for the full "contact time" it lists.
  • Wear gloves. Wash your hands for 20 seconds after.
  • Store cleaners out of reach of children and pets. Some cleaners can trigger asthma.

Bleach: follow the bottle first. If it has no directions, the CDC says mix 5 tablespoons (1/3 cup) of bleach in 1 gallon of room-temperature water, or 4 teaspoons in 1 quart. Leave it on at least 1 minute. Make a fresh batch each day.

Never mix cleaners

Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner. The mix can make fumes that are dangerous to breathe. Use one product, rinse, then use the next. If someone breathes in fumes, get them to fresh air and call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. If they have trouble breathing, call 911.

Mold and laundry

Mold grows where it stays wet. Fix leaks fast and dry wet things within 24 to 48 hours. Keep indoor humidity below 60%, ideally 30% to 50%. Run the bathroom fan or open a window when you shower. You can clean a patch smaller than about 10 square feet (3 feet by 3 feet) yourself: scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then dry them. Moldy carpet or ceiling tiles may need to be thrown out. Don't paint over mold. Bigger jobs, or mold after a flood, may need a professional. Renters, tell your landlord in writing. See tenant rights.

Laundry: clothes sold in the US must have a care label that tells you how to clean them. Follow it, and you will ruin fewer clothes.

Everywhere

Soap and water work everywhere. Fresh air, dry rooms and keeping chemicals away from children are good rules in any home.

In the United States

Disinfectants sold in the US are registered with the EPA. Use them as the label says, since the label is the instructions. Never use a disinfectant on people or pets. Clothing makers must give care instructions on a label under a Federal Trade Commission rule.
