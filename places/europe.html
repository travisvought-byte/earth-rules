EARTH RULES · EUROPE

Europe.

112 works free from any phone in every European Union country.

Emergency numbers

112 reaches police, fire and ambulance across the EU
CountryNumber
Every EU country112
United Kingdom999 or 112

Save the number in your phone when you arrive. If it isn't listed here, the government of the country you're in lists it on its own site.

Some EU countries also keep their own older numbers, but 112 always works there.

If you're thinking about suicide or can't cope

  • 116 123 is set aside across the EU for emotional support lines.
  • UK and Ireland: Samaritans on 116 123, free from any phone, 24 hours a day.

For any other country, Find A Helpline (opens in a new tab) lists free, checked help lines in more than 150 countries. It's run by ThroughLine and backed by the International Association for Suicide Prevention.

Rights everyone has

In 1948 the United Nations agreed on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (opens in a new tab). It says every person, in every country, is born free and equal in dignity and rights. Countries don't all live up to it, but it's the common starting point.

If you're arrested away from home

You can ask the police to tell your country's consulate. They have to tell you that you can ask. This comes from a treaty almost every country has signed, the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations.

Rights in Europe

46 countries have signed the European Convention on Human Rights, including every EU country and the UK. If your own country's courts don't fix a rights problem, you can take it to the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg, France, and its rulings bind the country involved.
