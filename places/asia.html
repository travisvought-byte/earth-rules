If you're thinking about suicide or can't cope

Find A Helpline (opens in a new tab) lists free, checked help lines in more than 150 countries. It's run by ThroughLine and backed by the International Association for Suicide Prevention.

Rights everyone has

In 1948 the United Nations agreed on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (opens in a new tab). It says every person, in every country, is born free and equal in dignity and rights. Countries don't all live up to it, but it's the common starting point.

If you're arrested away from home

You can ask the police to tell your country's consulate. They have to tell you that you can ask. This comes from a treaty almost every country has signed, the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations.

Rights in Asia

Asia has no region-wide human rights treaty or court. Southeast Asian countries agreed the ASEAN Human Rights Declaration in 2012, but it's a statement, with no court behind it. Several Arab countries have joined the Arab Charter on Human Rights, which has a committee but no court yet. In most of Asia, your rights come from each country's own laws.

Asia at a glance

About 4.81 billion people lived in Asia in 2024, according to the United Nations. That is close to 6 out of every 10 people on Earth.

Mandarin Chinese has more native speakers than any other language in the world. Hindi is an official language of India's national government, and English is also used there. Asia has 19 of the world's 33 megacities, including the three largest cities in 2025: Jakarta, Dhaka and Tokyo.
