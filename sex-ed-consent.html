Sex ed and consent.

Sex should only happen when everyone clearly says yes, and you have the right to free or low-cost care to stay healthy.

BodyTopic 6 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: This is the plain facts on consent, birth control and STI testing, for teens and adults.

Why it matters: Knowing the rules and where to get care protects you and the people you are with.

Do this first

  1. Get a clear yes. Silence or "not saying no" is not a yes.
  2. Stop any time someone changes their mind, even partway through.
  3. Know that someone drunk, high, passed out or asleep cannot consent.
  4. Find free or low-cost birth control and testing at a family planning clinic.
  5. If someone hurt you, call the RAINN hotline at 800-656-4673.

Numbers to call

  • 800-656-4673RAINN Sexual Assault Hotline

What consent means

The federal Office on Women's Health puts it simply: consent is a clear "yes" to sexual activity.

  • Not saying "no" does not mean yes.
  • You can change your mind and stop at any time, even after things have started.
  • A person who is drunk, high, drugged, passed out or asleep cannot give consent.
  • A person under the age of consent cannot legally consent. That age is set by each state.

When in doubt, ask, and respect the answer.

Birth control and STI testing

Birth control and testing for sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are easy to get, and often free or low cost.

  • Title X family planning clinics offer birth control, pregnancy tests, and STI and HIV testing and treatment. They mainly serve people with low income or no insurance. There are more than 4,000 of them. Find one at reproductivehealthservices.gov (opens in a new tab).
  • CDC's GetTested at gettested.cdc.gov (opens in a new tab) finds HIV, STI and hepatitis testing near you. Many sites offer free or low-cost, confidential testing.

The CDC recommends HIV screening for everyone 13 to 64, and chlamydia and gonorrhea screening for sexually active women under 25. Ask a clinic which tests fit you.

If someone hurt you

It is not your fault. The RAINN National Sexual Assault Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 in English and Spanish. Call 800-656-4673, text HOPE to 64673, or chat at rainn.org (opens in a new tab). If you are in danger now, call 911. See help for more.

Everywhere

Everywhere, consent means a free, clear yes that can be taken back. Laws on the age of consent are different from country to country.

In the United States

In the US, the age of consent is set by each state. The Department of Health and Human Services funds Title X clinics, and the CDC runs the GetTested locator.

In Ohio

In Ohio, it is a crime for an adult 18 or older to have sexual conduct with someone who is 13, 14 or 15 (Ohio Revised Code 2907.04 (opens in a new tab)).
