Contact your representative.

Your representatives work for you, and a short, clear call from a voter in their district gets counted.

CivicTopic 5 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: Your representatives are the elected people who speak for your area in Congress, your state capitol and your town.

Why it matters: Offices keep track of what voters say, and they can help when you are stuck with a federal agency.

Do this first

  1. Find who represents you by your home address or ZIP code.
  2. Call the office nearest you, or use the contact form on their website.
  3. Say your name, that you are a constituent, and your ZIP code.
  4. Stick to one issue and name the bill number if there is one.
  5. Say exactly what you want them to do, then thank them.

Numbers to call

  • 202-224-3121US Capitol switchboard
  • 1-844-872-4681USAGov, any government question

Find who represents you

  • US House: enter your ZIP code at house.gov (opens in a new tab). Some ZIP codes cover more than one district, so you may need your full ZIP+4.
  • US Senate: every state has two senators. Find yours at senate.gov (opens in a new tab).
  • State and local: USA.gov (opens in a new tab) links to governors, state lawmakers, mayors and county officials.

A short script

Most calls are answered by staff. That's fine. They log what you say. Try this:

"Hi, my name is ____. I'm a constituent from ZIP code ____. I'm calling about ____. I'd like the [Senator or Representative] to ____. Thank you."

When you write or use a web form, include your mailing address so the office knows you live in the district. Offices often don't answer people from outside their area.

Show up in person

  • Town halls. Watch your lawmaker's website and social media for public meetings, and bring one short question.
  • Public comment. City councils, county boards and school boards hold public meetings. Many set aside time for people to speak.
  • Before you speak, write out your notes, keep it short and to the point, and practice once out loud.

Everywhere

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights says everyone has the right to take part in the government of their country, directly or through representatives they freely choose. Most countries have a way to find and write to your local member of parliament.

In the United States

The US Capitol switchboard at 202-224-3121 can connect you to any senator's or representative's office in Washington. Your lawmaker's local office is often easier to reach and handles help with federal agencies. For any government question, USAGov answers for free at 1-844-872-4681.

In Ohio

The Ohio House and Ohio Senate websites each have a "Who Represents Me?" tool. Type your home address to find your state representative at ohiohouse.gov (opens in a new tab) and your state senator at ohiosenate.gov (opens in a new tab).

Ohio law says meetings of public bodies, like city councils, county commissioners and school boards, are open to the public at all times. Check their website for the schedule and agenda.
