The world in numbers.

About 8.2 billion people share this planet, and here is how they are spread out.

World and cultureTopic 1 of 8

In 10 seconds

What it is: This is a short almanac of the world: how many people there are, where they live, what languages they speak and how long they live.

Why it matters: Knowing the big numbers helps you understand the news, other countries and your own place in the world.

Do this first

  1. Remember the big one: about 8.2 billion people lived on Earth in 2024.
  2. Know that more than half of all people live in Asia.
  3. Look up any country on the UN World Population Prospects website.
  4. Check the year on any number you read. Population numbers change every year.
  5. Visit each continent page to learn more about the places and people there.

How many people, and where

The United Nations counts the world's people and publishes the numbers in a report called World Population Prospects. Its 2024 edition says the world had about 8.2 billion people in 2024.

The UN groups places a little differently than a map of continents does. "Northern America" is mainly the United States and Canada. Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean and South America are counted together as "Latin America and the Caribbean."

India and China are far ahead of every other country. Together they hold more than a third of all people.

What comes next

The UN expects the world to keep growing for about 60 more years. Its 2024 report says the number of people will likely peak at about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s, then slip to about 10.2 billion by 2100.

People are living longer, too. Worldwide, life expectancy at birth reached 73.3 years in 2024. The UN expects it to reach about 77.4 years by 2054.

Cities, towns and the countryside

The UN's World Urbanization Prospects 2025 uses a new way to measure where people live. By that measure, in 2025:

  • 45 out of every 100 people live in cities. That is more than double the share in 1950.
  • 36 out of every 100 live in towns.
  • 19 out of every 100 live in rural areas.

There are 33 megacities, which are cities with 10 million people or more. 19 of them are in Asia. The biggest is Jakarta, Indonesia, with nearly 42 million people, followed by Dhaka, Bangladesh, and Tokyo, Japan.

Languages

Nobody knows the exact number of languages, so every count is an estimate. Ethnologue, a well-known language catalog, says about 7,164 languages are in use today. It also says about 40 out of every 100 languages are endangered, often with fewer than 1,000 speakers left.

A few languages are very large. Just 23 languages are spoken by more than half of all people. Mandarin Chinese has the most native speakers. English has the most speakers in total when you count people who learned it as a second language.

Everywhere

These numbers cover the whole planet. The UN's population estimates cover 237 countries and areas. You can look up any country yourself at the UN World Population Prospects website (opens in a new tab).

In the United States

The United States is the third most populous country, with about 347 million people in 2025 by the UN's count. Only India and China have more.
