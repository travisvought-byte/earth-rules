How to learn.

Learning is a skill, and a few simple habits work much better than rereading.

BrainTopic 1 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: This page covers study habits that research supports and free places to learn almost anything.

Why it matters: You'll keep learning your whole life, for jobs, money, health and fun, so learning well saves time.

Do this first

  1. Test yourself instead of rereading. Close the book and recall what you can.
  2. Spread study over several days instead of cramming the night before.
  3. Mix different kinds of problems in one session.
  4. Sleep well the night before and the night after you study.
  5. Explain it to someone else in your own words.

What works best

Researchers who reviewed many studies rated two habits highest:

  • Testing yourself. Use flashcards, practice quizzes or a blank page. Pulling a memory out makes it stronger.
  • Spacing. Study the same thing in two or more short sessions spread over days. Any spacing beats none.

Mixing topics or problem types in one session also helps. You learn to pick the right tool for each problem.

Rereading, highlighting and summarizing feel productive, but research rates them low on their own.

Sleep is part of studying

Sleep before learning gets your brain ready to make memories. Sleep after learning helps lock them in. Losing sleep can cut learning ability by as much as 40%. The night after you study matters at least as much as the night before. See body basics.

Free places to learn

  • Your public library. Books, audiobooks and help from librarians who are good at finding things.
  • Khan Academy (opens in a new tab), a nonprofit with free lessons in math, science, reading and more.
  • Community college for classes and job training. See work.
  • High school equivalency (GED) classes for adults.

Everywhere

How memory works is the same for everyone: you remember what you practice pulling back out, spread over time, with enough sleep. These habits help at any age and in any subject, from a new language to a trade.

In the United States

Ask your public library where free adult classes and tutoring meet near you.

In Ohio

Ohio Aspire (opens in a new tab) is free. It helps adults prepare for the high school equivalency test and build skills to get a job, earn more or start college or job training. The Ohio Department of Education and Workforce lists programs by county.
