Leaving abuse safely.

You deserve to be safe, and trained advocates can help you plan a way out that fits your life.

In 10 seconds

What it is: How to plan for safety and leave an abusive relationship with help.

Why it matters: Abuse can get worse when someone leaves, so a plan made with an advocate can save your life.

Do this first

  1. If you are in danger right now, call 911.
  2. Use a safe phone or computer. Your partner may be able to see your history.
  3. Call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat with the Hotline.
  4. Make a safety plan with an advocate before you leave.
  5. Hide copies of key papers, keys and some cash somewhere safe.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency
  • 1-800-799-7233National Domestic Violence Hotline, 24/7
  • Text START to 88788Text START to the Hotline

Stay safe online

Your phone and computer can be watched without you knowing. Browser history can never be fully erased, even in private mode.

  • Use a computer at a library, a friend's home or work.
  • Think about a cheap pay-as-you-go phone kept somewhere safe.
  • Make a new email account your partner doesn't know about.
  • GPS trackers can be hidden in a car, purse or phone.

Make a safety plan

Many abusers get more violent after their partner leaves. An advocate can help you plan the safest time and way to go. Common steps:

  • Pick safe people and places, and a code word that means "I need help."
  • Memorize key phone numbers in case your phone is taken.
  • Hide an extra car key.
  • Keep a journal of what happens, with dates, if it is safe to do so.

The Hotline is free and open 24/7/365: 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org (opens in a new tab). You can talk without giving your name.

What to take

  • IDs, birth certificates, Social Security cards and passports for you and your kids
  • Health insurance cards, medical records and medicines
  • Bank and money records, car title, lease or deed
  • Any protection order, with copies
  • Proof of abuse: threatening messages, police or medical reports, photos
  • Spare keys and a safe phone

Keep copies on a thumb drive or with someone you trust. If you lose papers, see papers.

Shelter and protection orders

You do not have to pay to stay at a domestic violence shelter. Food and child care there are usually free too. A protection order is a court order that can tell an abuser to stay away and stop contacting you. Local domestic violence programs can help you get one, and may help you find a free lawyer.

Everywhere

Abuse is never your fault. Planning with an advocate or someone you trust makes leaving safer.

In the United States

The National Domestic Violence Hotline also connects you to local shelters and programs. Teens and young adults can reach love is respect at 1-866-331-9474. See all hotlines.

In Ohio

In Ohio you can ask the Domestic Relations Court for a domestic violence civil protection order. File in person at the clerk's office in the county where you live, where the abuser lives, or where the abuse happened. The same day, a judge can hold an emergency hearing and issue an order that starts right away. A full hearing usually follows within 7 or 10 business days. You don't need a lawyer, and local advocates can help with forms and sometimes go to court with you. Free guides and forms are at Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab).
