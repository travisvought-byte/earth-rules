Insurance.

Insurance is a deal: you pay a little often so a big loss doesn't wreck you.

Money and papersTopic 6 of 12

In 10 seconds

What it is: Insurance is a contract where you pay a company regularly and it pays for certain losses if they happen.

Why it matters: One crash, fire, illness or lawsuit can cost more than most people can pay, and insurance shares that risk.

Do this first

  1. Learn four words: premium, deductible, copay and out-of-pocket maximum.
  2. If you drive, carry at least the liability insurance your state requires.
  3. If you rent, get renters insurance. Your landlord's policy does not cover your things.
  4. Get quotes from several companies using the same information.
  5. Read the policy before you sign, and keep a copy.

Numbers to call

  • 1-800-686-1526Ohio Dept. of Insurance consumer line

Four words to know

  • Premium: what you pay for the insurance, often every month. You pay it whether you use care or not.
  • Deductible: what you pay for covered care before the plan starts paying. With a $2,000 deductible, you pay the first $2,000.
  • Copay: a set amount, like $20, you pay for a covered service.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you pay in a year for covered, in-network care. After that, the plan pays 100% of covered care. Premiums don't count toward it.

The cheapest premium is not always the best deal. If you need a lot of care, a higher premium with a lower deductible can cost less overall. See coverage for Medicaid, Medicare, Marketplace plans and VA care.

The main kinds

  • Health: pays part of your doctor, hospital and drug costs. See coverage.
  • Car: liability pays for injuries and damage you cause to others. Collision fixes your car after a crash. Comprehensive covers theft, fire, weather and broken glass. Uninsured motorist pays for your injuries if a driver with no insurance hits you.
  • Renters: pays to replace your things if they are stolen or destroyed, and covers you if someone gets hurt in your home.
  • Homeowners: covers the house, your things and injuries to guests. Lenders usually require it. Floods and earthquakes are not covered by a typical policy.
  • Life: pays your family if you die. Term covers a set number of years and costs less at first. Whole life lasts your whole life, builds cash value and costs more.
  • Disability: pays part of your income if illness or injury keeps you from working. A typical policy pays about 60% of your pay.

Shopping and problems

  • Get quotes from several companies. Give each one the same information.
  • Check that the company and agent are licensed with your state insurance department.
  • Ask which discounts you qualify for.
  • Answer the application honestly and fully, and keep a copy.
  • Don't cancel an old policy until the new one is in force.
  • For life insurance, make checks out to the insurance company, not the agent.

Having a problem? Call the company first and ask for a supervisor if needed. If that fails, file a complaint with your state insurance department. It's free.

Everywhere

Insurance works by pooling. Many people each pay a small amount, and the money pays for the few who have a big loss. The basic ideas of premium, deductible and coverage limits are the same in most countries.

In the United States

Every state has an insurance department that licenses companies and agents and takes complaints. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (opens in a new tab) has free consumer guides and links to every state's department.

Most states require car insurance to drive legally. The amounts are different in each state.

In Ohio

Ohio law says you must carry insurance to drive. The minimums are:

  • $25,000 for injury or death of one person
  • $50,000 for injury or death of two or more people
  • $25,000 for property damage in one crash

You must show proof at traffic stops, crash scenes and vehicle inspections. These are the bare minimums. A serious crash can cost much more.

The Ohio Department of Insurance (opens in a new tab) takes complaints about insurance companies and agents. Consumer line: 1-800-686-1526.
