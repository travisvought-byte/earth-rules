Parenting by age.

Every stage of a child's life needs something a little different from you, and you don't have to know it all at once.

PeopleTopic 7 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: A short guide to what matters most at each age, from a new baby to a teenager.

Why it matters: Kids change fast, and a few simple habits like checkups, safe sleep and car seats protect them while they grow.

Do this first

  1. Put your baby to sleep on their back, alone, on a firm flat surface.
  2. Keep every well-child checkup, even when your child seems healthy.
  3. Use the right car seat for your child's size, and keep kids under 13 in the back seat.
  4. Track milestones with the free CDC checklists and tell the doctor if something worries you.
  5. Call a parent helpline when you're stressed. Asking for help is part of the job.

Numbers to call

  • 1-855-427-2736National Parent & Youth Helpline, 24/7
  • 1-800-755-4769Ohio Help Me Grow (1-800-755-GROW)

Babies (birth to 1)

Safe sleep saves lives. The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign says:

  • Place your baby on their back for every sleep until age 1.
  • Use a firm, flat sleep surface with only a fitted sheet. No pillows, blankets, bumpers or toys.
  • Keep baby's bed in your room, near your bed but not in it. Couches and armchairs are never safe for baby to sleep on.
  • Keep baby away from smoke and vape, and don't let baby get too hot.
  • Give supervised tummy time while baby is awake.

Car seats: babies under 1 always ride rear-facing.

Toddlers and preschool (1 to 5)

  • Milestones: CDC's "Learn the Signs. Act Early." program has free milestone checklists and a free app for ages birth to 5. If you have a concern, talk with the doctor and ask for a developmental screening. Don't wait and see.
  • Car seats: keep your child rear-facing as long as the seat allows, then use a forward-facing seat with a harness until they reach the seat's limit.
  • Checkups come often: at 12, 15, 18, 24 and 30 months, then once a year.

School age and teens

  • Checkups: the American Academy of Pediatrics lists one well-child visit every year from age 3 through 21. These visits cover vaccines, growth and any worries you or your child have.
  • Booster seat until the seat belt fits right: lap belt across the upper thighs, shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.
  • Back seat at least through age 12.
  • Teens still need you. Keep talking, listen more than you lecture, and see turning 18 for what changes at that age.

When you need a break

Parenting is hard. The National Parent & Youth Helpline is free, 24 hours a day. Call, text or chat at 1-855-427-2736. Parents, caregivers and kids can all use it. For food, child care and other help, see basics.

Everywhere

Babies everywhere are safest sleeping on their backs on a firm, flat surface with nothing else in the bed. Regular checkups and vaccines help children grow up healthy in every country.

In the United States

Certified car seat technicians will check your car seat for free in most cases. NHTSA lists inspection stations at nhtsa.gov (opens in a new tab). CDC milestone checklists are free at cdc.gov (opens in a new tab). For coverage of checkups, see health coverage.

In Ohio

Help Me Grow is Ohio's front door for young families. One referral can connect you to Early Intervention, home visiting, Family Connects and WIC. Early Intervention helps children from birth to age 3 who have developmental delays or disabilities, in the places your family already spends time. Anyone can make a referral, and Help Me Grow contacts the parent first. Call 1-800-755-GROW or refer online at ochids.childrenandyouth.ohio.gov (opens in a new tab).
