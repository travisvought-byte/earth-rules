Deism.

A fair, plain look.

Deists believe in one God, the creator, known through nature and reason rather than through holy books.

In their own words

Deism is belief in God based on nature and reason, not on a religion. The World Union of Deists describes it as seeing a universal creative force greater than people, by watching the laws and design of nature and using human reason. Deists do not accept claims that God gave special messages to prophets or through holy books. Many see God as a creator who set the universe in motion, like a clockmaker. Deism grew in England and Europe in the 1600s and 1700s. In America, Thomas Paine, Benjamin Franklin and Thomas Jefferson held deist views.

What they believe

  • One God created the universe and its natural laws.
  • Reason and nature, not holy books, show us God.
  • Claims of special messages from God are not accepted.
  • Live a good life and follow your conscience.
  • Many deists see Jesus as a great moral teacher, not as God.

What they do

  • Study nature and think things through for themselves.
  • Practice on their own, without clergy, scriptures or rituals.
  • Try to live by conscience and do what is right.
  • Some connect with other deists in online and regional groups.

How it answers the quiz

How Deism answers each question
QuestionAnswer
What do you think about God?There is one God who cares about people. Some also say: The divine is in everything, or behind all things.
Who was Jesus?One of many wise teachers. Some also say: He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?No one can know. Some also say: We are judged, then heaven or hell.
What do you trust most to know what's true?Reason, evidence and science.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Just being a good person. Nothing needs saving.
How much do you want a community?Mostly on my own.
Who should lead a faith community?No clergy at all.
How many daily rules feel right to you?Very few.
How would you rather connect?I don't need prayer or ritual.
What about other beliefs?No spiritual path is needed. Some also say: Many paths can lead to the truth.
What is nature to you?Made by God, and ours to care for.
