EARTH RULES · UNITARIAN UNIVERSALISM

Unitarian Universalism.

A fair, plain look.

Unitarian Universalists are a faith community with no shared creed, held together by shared values centered on love.

In their own words

Unitarian Universalists (often called UUs) belong to a faith with no shared creed. Members can be theists, atheists, agnostics, or anything in between. Some also call themselves Christian, Jewish, Buddhist, Pagan or Humanist. What holds them together is a covenant, a set of shared promises, with love at the center. Their six shared values are interdependence, pluralism, justice, transformation, generosity and equity. UUs learn from many world religions and philosophies. Each congregation runs itself, and members vote on things like calling their minister. Many UUs work for justice and for the care of the Earth.

  • How manyAbout 130,000 members in about 1,000 US congregations (the UUA says)2024 · Unitarian Universalist Association: UUA Membership Statistics, 1961 to 2024 (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • There is no shared creed; each person searches for truth and meaning.
  • Love is the power that holds the community together.
  • Every person is worthy and has the right to live with dignity.
  • All life is part of an interdependent web of existence.
  • Views on God and life after death differ; many hold them as open questions.

What they do

  • Gather for Sunday worship, which often starts by lighting a flaming chalice.
  • Share music, readings, a sermon, and the joys and concerns of members.
  • Offer faith learning programs for children and youth during Sunday worship.
  • Use many spiritual practices, such as meditation, prayer or journaling.
  • Work together for justice and to protect the Earth.

How it answers the quiz

How Unitarian Universalism answers each question
QuestionAnswer
What do you think about God?We can't really know, or it doesn't matter. Some also say: There is one God who cares about people; The divine is in everything, or behind all things; There is no god.
Who was Jesus?One of many wise teachers. Some also say: He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?Nothing. This life is all there is; No one can know.
What do you trust most to know what's true?What I feel inside, my conscience; Reason, evidence and science.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Just being a good person. Nothing needs saving.
How much do you want a community?I want to gather with others every week.
Who should lead a faith community?Each community chooses its own leaders.
How many daily rules feel right to you?Very few.
How would you rather connect?Meditate, or sit in quiet thought. Some also say: Pray, talking to God; Ceremonies and offerings.
What about other beliefs?Many paths can lead to the truth.
What is nature to you?Sacred in itself. The seasons matter. Some also say: Nature. Science explains it.
