EARTH RULES · JAINISM

Jainism.

A fair, plain look.

Jains follow an ancient Indian path of nonviolence toward every living being, shown by 24 great teachers called Tirthankaras.

In their own words

Jainism is one of the oldest religions of India that is still practiced. Jains believe every living being, from people to animals to plants, has a soul called a jiva. Souls are caught in a long cycle of rebirth. Karma is like a fine material that sticks to the soul and clouds it. The goal is moksha, setting the soul free. Jains do not believe in a creator god or a moment of creation. They honor 24 Tirthankaras, souls who set themselves free and showed the way. Mahavira was the last. Ahimsa, or nonviolence, guides every part of Jain life. The two main groups are the Digambara and the Shvetambara.

  • How manyAbout 4.5 million in India (census count)2011 · Pew Research Center: Key findings about the religious composition of India (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • Every living being, even a plant, has a soul.
  • Ahimsa, or nonviolence toward all living things, guides every choice.
  • There is no creator god; each person must free their own soul.
  • The Tirthankaras are freed souls who teach the way, not gods who grant favors.
  • Right vision, right knowledge and right conduct lead to freedom.

What they do

  • Eating a vegetarian diet so as not to harm living beings.
  • Going to the temple to pray, meditate, sing and hear monks teach.
  • In many groups, offering flowers, water and incense before images of the Tirthankaras.
  • Keeping vows of nonviolence, truth, not stealing, chastity and not clinging to things.
  • Marking festivals such as Paryushana, a time of forgiveness.

How it answers the quiz

How Jainism answers each question
QuestionAnswer
What do you think about God?There is no god. Some also say: There are many gods or divine beings.
Who was Jesus?He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?We are born again and again until we are freed.
What do you trust most to know what's true?A holy book plus the teaching of wise leaders over time.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Meditation, discipline and seeing clearly.
How much do you want a community?On holidays and big life events, and at home the rest. Some also say: I want to gather with others every week.
Who should lead a faith community?Each community chooses its own leaders. Some also say: No clergy at all.
How many daily rules feel right to you?Many: food, clothes, prayer times. Some also say: A few set practices.
How would you rather connect?Ceremonies and offerings; Meditate, or sit in quiet thought.
What about other beliefs?Many paths can lead to the truth. Some also say: There is one true path.
What is nature to you?Sacred in itself. The seasons matter.
