EARTH RULES · SHINTO

Shinto.

A fair, plain look.

Shinto, "the way of the kami," is the traditional religion of Japan, centered on honoring sacred spirits called kami.

In their own words

Shinto is the native religion of Japan. Its name means "the way of the kami." Kami are sacred spirits with awe-inspiring power. There are countless kami. They include the sun goddess Amaterasu, great ancestors, and parts of nature such as plants, rocks and animals. Shinto has no founder, no official holy book and no fixed creed. What matters most are rituals that honor the kami and keep people pure. People visit shrines, join festivals called matsuri, and keep small home altars. Shinto teaches that the soul lives on after death, and families honor their ancestors. Many people in Japan take part in both Shinto and Buddhism.

  • How manyAbout 85 million in Japan, as counted by Shinto groups themselves (many are also counted as Buddhist)2016 · Nippon.com: Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs religion figures (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • Kami, sacred spirits, live in nature, in ancestors and in great people.
  • Purity matters; uncleanness is washed away through purification rites.
  • Living in step with the will of the kami brings their protection.
  • The soul lives on after death, and ancestors stay close to their families.
  • Shinto can be practiced side by side with other religions, like Buddhism.

What they do

  • Visiting a local shrine to pay respects and ask for blessings like health.
  • Keeping a kamidana, a home altar, with food offerings morning and evening.
  • Joining shrine festivals (matsuri), often with portable shrines carried in parades.
  • Making offerings to honor family ancestors.
  • Holding Shinto funerals at home, not at the shrine.

What do you think about God?There are many gods or divine beings. Some also say: The divine is in everything, or behind all things.
Who was Jesus?He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?Our spirit lives on with the ancestors. Some also say: No one can know.
What do you trust most to know what's true?A holy book plus the teaching of wise leaders over time. Some also say: What I feel inside, my conscience.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Just being a good person. Nothing needs saving. Some also say: Meditation, discipline and seeing clearly.

How would you rather connect?Ceremonies and offerings. Some also say: Pray, talking to God.
What about other beliefs?Many paths can lead to the truth.
What is nature to you?Sacred in itself. The seasons matter.
