EARTH RULES · SIKHISM

Sikhism.

A fair, plain look.

Sikhs follow the teachings of Guru Nanak and the nine Gurus after him, and the holy book Guru Granth Sahib, the living Guru.

In their own words

Sikhs believe in one God, without form, who made all things. The faith began with Guru Nanak in the Punjab region of South Asia in the late 1400s. Nine more Gurus followed. The tenth, Guru Gobind Singh, passed the Guru's light to the holy book, the Guru Granth Sahib, which Sikhs honor as their eternal Guru. Sikhs believe souls are born again and again, and that remembering God's name sets a person free from this cycle. Guru Nanak taught that all people are equal, whatever their caste, class or gender. Sikhs aim to remember God, work honestly and share with others.

  • How manyAbout 25 million worldwideearly 2000s · Britannica: Sikhism (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • There is one God (Ik Onkar), without form, who made all things.
  • The Guru Granth Sahib is the living, eternal Guru.
  • Souls are reborn until set free by remembering God's name.
  • All people are equal, whatever their caste, class or gender.

What they do

  • Remember God through prayer and meditation on God's name.
  • Earn an honest living and share with people in need.
  • Gather at the gurdwara to sing hymns from the Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Serve and eat the free community meal (langar), sitting together as equals.
  • Initiated Sikhs (Khalsa) keep the five Ks, such as uncut hair and a steel bracelet.

How it answers the quiz

How Sikhism answers each question
QuestionAnswer
What do you think about God?There is one God who cares about people. Some also say: The divine is in everything, or behind all things.
Who was Jesus?He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?We are born again and again until we are freed.
What do you trust most to know what's true?A holy book. Some also say: A holy book plus the teaching of wise leaders over time.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Meditation, discipline and seeing clearly. Some also say: Just being a good person. Nothing needs saving.
How much do you want a community?I want to gather with others every week. Some also say: On holidays and big life events, and at home the rest.
Who should lead a faith community?Each community chooses its own leaders. Some also say: No clergy at all.
How many daily rules feel right to you?A few set practices. Some also say: Many: food, clothes, prayer times.
How would you rather connect?Pray, talking to God; Meditate, or sit in quiet thought.
What about other beliefs?Many paths can lead to the truth.
What is nature to you?Made by God, and ours to care for.
