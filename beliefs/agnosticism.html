Agnosticism.

A fair, plain look.

Agnostics hold that we cannot know, or do not yet know, whether God exists.

In their own words

Agnosticism is the view that people cannot know whether God, or anything beyond what we can experience, exists. The word was first used in 1869 by the British scientist Thomas Henry Huxley. He called it not a creed but a method: follow reason as far as it goes, then admit what we do not know. American Atheists puts it this way: atheism is about what you believe, and agnosticism is about what you know. Agnostics hold many views. Pew Research Center found that 43% of US agnostics believe in God or a universal spirit, and 55% believe in something spiritual beyond the natural world.

  • How manyAbout 6% of US adults (Pew Research Center)2023-24 · Pew Research Center: Agnostics, 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • We cannot know for sure whether God exists.
  • Follow reason and evidence as far as they go.
  • Be honest about the limits of what we know.
  • Big questions, like what happens after death, stay open.

What they do

  • Keep asking questions and weigh the evidence.
  • Most do not belong to a religion.
  • Make choices using reason and their own conscience.
  • Some join secular community groups, like local humanist groups or Sunday Assemblies.
