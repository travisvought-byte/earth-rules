EARTH RULES · JUDAISM

Reform and liberal Judaism.

Part of the Judaism family.

Reform Jews hold to God, Torah and the Jewish people, and believe Judaism can grow and change with each new time.

In their own words

Reform Jews affirm the central teachings of Judaism: God, Torah and Israel. They believe in one God, even if members understand God in different ways. They see the Torah as God's ongoing revelation to the Jewish people, open to new understanding over time. Each person studies Jewish teaching and chooses how to take part in it. Reform Jews are called to be God's partners in tikkun olam, which means repairing the world through justice and kindness. The movement began in Germany over 200 years ago. It embraces religious and cultural diversity and seeks dialogue with people of other faiths.

  • How manyAbout 37% of Jewish adults in the U.S.2020 · Pew Research Center: Jewish Americans in 2020 (opens in a new tab)

What they believe

  • There is one God, though members may understand God in different ways.
  • The Torah is God's ongoing revelation, open to new understanding.
  • People are God's partners in repairing the world (tikkun olam).
  • The spirit within us is eternal.
  • We help bring nearer a time of peace for all, the messianic age.

What they do

  • Gather at the temple or synagogue for Shabbat services.
  • Keep the Jewish holy days and mark life events with the congregation.
  • Study Torah and Hebrew.
  • Pray to God in public and in private.
  • Take part in social justice work and service to the community.

How it answers the quiz

How Reform and liberal Judaism answers each question
QuestionAnswer
What do you think about God?There is one God who cares about people. Some also say: We can't really know, or it doesn't matter.
Who was Jesus?He isn't part of what I believe.
What happens after we die?No one can know. Some also say: Our spirit lives on with the ancestors; Nothing. This life is all there is.
What do you trust most to know what's true?A holy book plus the teaching of wise leaders over time. Some also say: What I feel inside, my conscience; Reason, evidence and science.
What matters most for a good life, or after it?Just being a good person. Nothing needs saving. Some also say: Following God's law and doing what is right.
How much do you want a community?On holidays and big life events, and at home the rest. Some also say: I want to gather with others every week.
Who should lead a faith community?Each community chooses its own leaders.
How many daily rules feel right to you?Very few. Some also say: A few set practices.
How would you rather connect?Pray, talking to God. Some also say: Meditate, or sit in quiet thought.
What about other beliefs?Many paths can lead to the truth.
What is nature to you?Made by God, and ours to care for.
