Identity theft.

If someone uses your name or numbers, there's a free, step-by-step way to fix it.

Money and papersTopic 8 of 12

In 10 seconds

What it is: Identity theft is when someone uses your personal information, like your Social Security number, to open accounts, file taxes or get benefits.

Why it matters: It can hurt your credit, your tax refund and your benefits, and acting fast limits the damage.

Do this first

  1. Go to IdentityTheft.gov to report it and get a recovery plan.
  2. Freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. It's free.
  3. Check your credit reports at AnnualCreditReport.com.
  4. Call any company where a fake account was opened.
  5. Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN to protect your tax return.

Numbers to call

  • 1-877-322-8228Free credit reports
  • 1-877-382-4357FTC
  • 1-800-282-0515Ohio Attorney General

Signs it happened

  • Accounts or credit checks on your credit report you don't recognize
  • Charges or withdrawals you didn't make
  • An IRS letter saying more than one tax return was filed in your name, or income from a job you don't have
  • Medical bills or Explanation of Benefits for care you didn't get
  • A letter about unemployment benefits you never applied for

Freeze and fraud alerts

A credit freeze stops new lenders from seeing your credit report, so thieves can't open new accounts. It's free to place and lift, it doesn't hurt your credit score, and it lasts until you lift it. You must contact all three bureaus: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion.

A fraud alert tells lenders to check it's really you. Contact one bureau and it tells the other two. It lasts one year. Identity theft victims can get an extended alert that lasts seven years.

You can check each credit report for free every week at AnnualCreditReport.com (opens in a new tab), the only official site. See credit.

Protect your taxes

An IRS Identity Protection PIN is a six-digit number that stops anyone else from filing a tax return with your Social Security number. Anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can prove who they are can get one. You get a new one each year. The fastest way is your online account at IRS.gov (opens in a new tab).

The IRS will never ask for your IP PIN by phone, email or text. Anyone who does is a scammer. See taxes.

Everywhere

Anywhere you live, treat your ID numbers like keys. Give them out only when you must, shred papers with them on, and turn on alerts from your bank so you see new charges right away.

In the United States

IdentityTheft.gov (opens in a new tab), run by the Federal Trade Commission, lets you report identity theft and get a personal recovery plan. It also creates an Identity Theft Report you can use to prove to businesses that you were a victim. FTC: 1-877-382-4357.

Some kinds of help, like Ohio's below, need a police report. Bring your ID and your FTC report to your local police.

In Ohio

The Ohio Attorney General's Identity Theft Unit (opens in a new tab) can work with credit bureaus, banks and collectors for you. You'll need a police report and the office's identity theft affidavit form. Call 1-800-282-0515.

If someone claimed unemployment benefits in your name, report it to the Ohio Department of Job and Family Services at 833-658-0394.
