Home emergencies.

A few minutes of planning now can save your life when a fire, flood or outage hits.

HomeTopic 3 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: These are the most common home emergencies: a house fire, rising water, and losing power.

Why it matters: In a fire you may have less than 2 minutes to get out, so you need to know what to do before it happens.

Do this first

  1. Put smoke alarms on every level of your home and test them every month.
  2. Find 2 ways out of every room and pick a meeting spot outside.
  3. In a fire, get out, stay out, and call 911 from outside.
  4. Never drive or walk into flood water. Turn around.
  5. Run a generator only outside, at least 20 feet from doors and windows.
  6. Pack a go-bag so you can leave fast.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency
  • 1-800-686-7826PUCO, Ohio utility outages

Fire

  • Smoke alarms go on every level, including the basement, inside every bedroom, and outside sleeping areas. Test them every month. Replace any alarm 10 years old or older.
  • Carbon monoxide alarms help too. Test them every month.
  • Make a plan. Find 2 ways out of every room. Pick a meeting place in front of your home. Practice with everyone who lives there. You could have less than 2 minutes to escape once the alarm sounds.
  • If there's a fire, get out fast and stay out. Never go back in for people, pets or things. Call 911 from outside on a cell phone or a neighbor's phone.
  • On the way out, feel the doorknob and the cracks around the door. If it's hot, keep it closed and use your other way out. Stay low under smoke. Close doors behind you.

Flood

Turn around, don't drown. Just 6 inches of fast-moving water can knock over an adult. Twelve inches of water can float a small car. You can't tell how deep the water is or if the road under it is gone. Never drive or walk into flood water. Go to higher ground and follow local alerts.

Power outage

  • Report it. Tell your electric company right away, by phone, website, text or app.
  • Stay away from downed wires. They can kill. Call 911.
  • Generators make carbon monoxide. Never use one inside your home, garage or carport. Keep it outside, at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents.
  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A closed fridge keeps food safe for up to 4 hours. A full freezer holds about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24 hours. After 4 hours without power, throw out meat, poultry, fish, eggs and leftovers from the fridge. Never taste food to check it. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • Protect your stuff. Unplug computers and TVs. Leave one lamp on so you know when power is back.
  • Medical equipment at home? Ask your electric company about its program for customers with medical needs, and keep a backup plan.

Get ready ahead of time with a go-bag.

Everywhere

The same rules save lives in any country: a working smoke alarm, two ways out, never going back into a burning building, staying out of flood water, and never running a fuel engine indoors. Learn your country's emergency number.

In the United States

Call 911 for fire, flooding with people in danger, or downed power lines. The US Fire Administration, the National Weather Service and FoodSafety.gov all have free guides for getting ready.

In Ohio

Report outages to your electric company first. If you have frequent or long outages, report them to the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) at 1-800-686-7826. Ohio utilities have a program for customers who need power for medical equipment. Your doctor has to confirm the need. Being on the list does not promise fast repair, so have a backup plan.
