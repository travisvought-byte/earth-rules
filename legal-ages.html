Legal ages.

The law says when you are old enough to work, drive, vote and more.

Your timelineTopic 6 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: Legal ages are the birthdays when the law lets you do something new, like get a job, drive, vote or sign a contract.

Why it matters: Knowing these ages helps you plan, keeps you out of trouble and helps you protect the young people in your life.

Do this first

  1. Find your age in the table below to see what you can do now.
  2. If you are 14 to 17 and want a job in Ohio, get a work permit first.
  3. Start your Ohio driver's permit at 15 and a half.
  4. Register to vote at 17 if you will be 18 by the November election.
  5. Carry a photo ID when you buy alcohol, tobacco or cannabis.

Work rules for teens

Federal law: 16 is the basic minimum age for most jobs. At 14 and 15 you can work outside school hours, but only:

  • 3 hours on a school day, and 18 hours in a school week.
  • 8 hours on a day without school, and 40 hours in a week without school.
  • Between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. From June 1 through Labor Day, you can work until 9 p.m.

At 16 and 17 you can work any hours, but not in jobs the government calls hazardous. Those wait until 18.

When federal and state rules differ, the stricter rule wins. See work and workers' rights.

Driving in Ohio

  • At 15 and a half, you can take the knowledge test and get a temporary permit.
  • Hold the permit at least 6 months. Finish driver's ed (24 hours of class and 8 hours of driving with an instructor) and 50 hours of practice, with at least 10 at night.
  • At 16, you can get a probationary license.
  • Until 18, you can't use a phone while driving, and there are limits on late-night driving and passengers.

See car for buying, insuring and caring for a vehicle.

Sex, consent and marriage

In Ohio, an adult 18 or older who has sex with someone 13, 14 or 15 commits a crime (Ohio Revised Code 2907.04). In practice, that makes 16 the age of consent with an adult. Consent always means a clear, free yes. See sex ed and consent.

Ohio's marriage age is 18. A 17-year-old can marry only with a juvenile court's consent, and the other person can be no more than 4 years older.

Everywhere

Legal ages are different in every country. The Inter-Parliamentary Union says the voting age is 18 in most countries, though a few let younger people vote. Drinking ages vary much more. The World Health Organization found that 18 is the most common minimum age to buy alcohol, followed by 21 and 16, and some countries have no minimum at all. When you travel, check the local rules.

In the United States

Some ages are set for the whole country:

  • Voting: The 26th Amendment, ratified in 1971, protects the right to vote for citizens 18 and older. See voting.
  • Alcohol: 21. Before the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984, the drinking age could differ from state to state.
  • Tobacco and vapes: 21, under a federal law signed December 20, 2019. It covers tobacco products, including e-cigarettes and e-liquids.
  • Jury duty: You must be at least 18 to serve on a federal jury. See jury duty.
  • Military: 17 with a parent's consent, 18 without.
  • Retirement: Social Security can start at 62. Full retirement age is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later. Medicare is for people 65 or older. See coverage.

In Ohio

  • Adulthood: At 18 you are an adult under Ohio law and can sign contracts.
  • Work permit: Every minor age 14 through 17 needs a work permit. No minor can work more than 5 hours in a row without a 30-minute break.
  • Teen hours: Under 16, Ohio follows the same hour limits as federal law. Since October 9, 2026, Ohio also lets you work until 9 p.m. the night before a day with no school, if a parent says yes. Federal law can still be stricter. At 16 and 17, you can't start before 7 a.m. on a school day or work past 11 p.m. on a school night.
  • Voting at 17: If you will be 18 by the general election, you can register and vote in that year's primary to choose candidates. You can't vote on issues until you are 18.
  • Cannabis: Adult-use cannabis is legal only for people 21 and older, and stores must check a government photo ID.
