Dating red flags.

Love should feel safe and equal, so it helps to know the signs that something is off.

Love and familyTopic 2 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: The warning signs that a relationship is becoming controlling or abusive, and the signs that it is healthy.

Why it matters: Control often starts small and sweet, and spotting it early makes it much easier to get out or set limits.

Do this first

  1. Trust your gut. If something feels wrong, take it seriously.
  2. Slow things down if a new partner rushes you.
  3. Keep your friends and family close, no matter what a partner says.
  4. Never send money to someone you haven't met in person.
  5. Talk to a free advocate at love is respect, 24/7.

Numbers to call

  • 1-866-331-9474love is respect, call
  • Text LOVEIS to 22522love is respect, text LOVEIS

Red flags

  • Too much, too fast. Big gifts, nonstop compliments, "I love you" within days. Sweet behavior can be a tactic to get you to lower your guard.
  • Cutting you off. They don't want you talking to or hanging out with other people.
  • Jealousy and control. They tell you what to wear or use jealousy as an excuse to control you.
  • Watching your phone. Checking your texts, likes or messages. No partner has the right to monitor you online.
  • Pressure. They don't take no for an answer. Someone who ignores your no early on is likely to keep doing it.

Green flags

  • You can talk openly about good and bad feelings, and they listen.
  • They respect your opinions, your needs and your limits.
  • You trust each other and don't have to "prove" anything.
  • You make decisions together.
  • Time apart and time with friends is normal and fine.
  • Both people freely agree to anything sexual.

Online love and money

Romance scammers say it's true love but can never meet. They often claim to be overseas, in the military or on an oil rig. Then they ask for money for a medical bill, a ticket or a fee, often by gift card, wire, app or crypto. The FTC's rule: never send money or gifts to someone you haven't met in person. More on scams.

If you're worried

You can end any relationship at any time for any reason. If it's safe, keep a private record of what happens. Talk to an advocate at love is respect: call 1-866-331-9474, text LOVEIS to 22522, or chat at loveisrespect.org (opens in a new tab). See also manipulation.

Everywhere

Healthy love is built on respect, trust, honesty and equal say. Control, isolation and pressure are warning signs in any culture.

In the United States

love is respect is the national helpline for dating abuse. It serves teens, young adults and people worried about someone. Report romance scams to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (opens in a new tab) and to the app or site where you met.
