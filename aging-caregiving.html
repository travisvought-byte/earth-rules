Aging and caregiving.

Getting older and caring for someone you love both go better with a plan and some help.

Love and familyTopic 9 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: This is where to find local help for older adults, the papers to fill out ahead of time, and how caregivers can get a break.

Why it matters: Most families face this at some point, and free help exists that many people never hear about.

Do this first

  1. Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 to find local help.
  2. Fill out a health care power of attorney and a living will.
  3. Give copies to your proxy, your doctors and your family.
  4. Learn the basics of Medicare before turning 65.
  5. If you are a caregiver, ask for help before you are worn out.

Numbers to call

  • 1-800-677-1116Eldercare Locator
  • 1-866-243-5678Ohio Area Agency on Aging

Find local help

The Eldercare Locator (opens in a new tab) is a free federal service. Call or text 1-800-677-1116, or chat on the site. It connects you to your local Area Agency on Aging.

Area Agencies on Aging help older adults stay in their own homes. Help can include home-delivered meals, help around the house, rides, caregiver support, and benefits advice.

Papers to fill out now

The National Institute on Aging explains these:

  • Living will: tells doctors what treatment you want, or do not want, if you cannot speak for yourself.
  • Health care power of attorney: names a person (a proxy) to make medical choices for you if you cannot.
  • Power of attorney for finances: names someone to handle money matters if you cannot.

You do not need a lawyer for a health care directive. Most states have free forms. A lawyer or legal aid can help with a financial power of attorney. Review your papers once a year and after big life changes.

Medicare in brief

Medicare is health insurance for people 65 and older, and some younger people with disabilities. Part A covers hospital stays. Part B covers doctor visits. Part D covers prescriptions. See coverage for more.

Caring for the caregiver

Warning signs of caregiver stress include feeling worn out, angry, alone or hopeless, poor sleep, and skipping your own care. Do not wait until you are overwhelmed.

  • Ask for small, specific help. A list lets people pick what they can do.
  • Respite care gives you a break. It can be a friend, an in-home aide for a few hours, or an adult day program.
  • Your Area Agency on Aging can point you to caregiver support.

Everywhere

Everywhere, it helps to write down your wishes, name someone you trust to speak for you, and talk with your family before a crisis.

In the United States

In the US, the Administration for Community Living runs the Eldercare Locator. Rules for advance directives differ by state, so use your state's forms.

In Ohio

Ohio has 12 Area Agencies on Aging that cover the whole state. Call 1-866-243-5678 to be connected to yours. They offer meals, home repairs, help with heating and cooling costs, caregiver support, and free long-term care consultations.
