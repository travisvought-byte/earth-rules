AI fakes.

Computers can now fake a voice, a face or a video, so check the source, not just your eyes and ears.

BrainTopic 5 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: AI fakes, or deepfakes, are voices, pictures and videos made by computer programs to look and sound real.

Why it matters: Scammers use them to pretend to be your family, your boss or officials so you'll send money fast.

Do this first

  1. Pick a secret family code word to prove who's calling.
  2. If a loved one calls in trouble, hang up and call them back at a number you know.
  3. Never send gift cards, wire transfers or crypto because of a scary call.
  4. Check a surprising video or photo with trusted news or official sources.
  5. Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Numbers to call

  • 1-877-382-4357FTC
  • 1-800-282-0515Ohio Attorney General

The fake family emergency

A scammer needs only a short clip of someone's voice, often from a video online, to clone it. Then you get a call that sounds like your grandchild, child or friend in trouble. They say it's urgent. They say keep it secret. They want money now by wire, gift card, payment app or crypto.

  • Hang up. Call the person back at a number you know is theirs.
  • Can't reach them? Call another family member or friend, even if the caller said not to.
  • Ask a question only the real person would know, or ask for your family code word. The FBI recommends setting one.

Fake photos and videos

Fakes can have odd hands or feet, strange teeth or eyes, weird shadows, or lag and jerky movement. But good fakes may have none of these. Check the source, not the pixels.

  • Who posted it first? Is it on trusted news or an official site?
  • Do a reverse image search. Most big search engines let you search with a picture. It can show where the photo first appeared and if it came from a different event.
  • Pause when a post makes you very angry or scared. That's when fakes work best. See critical thinking.

AI chatbots can be wrong

AI chat tools can state false things with total confidence. Experts call these errors "confabulations" or "hallucinations." Use a chatbot as a starting point, then check anything important, like health, money or legal facts, with a real source or a professional.

Everywhere

The same tricks are used worldwide: a familiar voice or face, a sudden emergency, a demand for secrecy and fast payment. Slowing down and checking another way beats almost every one of them.

In the United States

Report scams to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (opens in a new tab) or 1-877-382-4357. If you lost money online, also report it to the FBI at ic3.gov (opens in a new tab). Type that address yourself. The real IC3 never charges to recover lost money. See scams.

In Ohio

The FTC says to report family emergency scams to your state attorney general too. In Ohio, call the Ohio Attorney General (opens in a new tab) at 1-800-282-0515.
