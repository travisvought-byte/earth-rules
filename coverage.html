Health coverage.

What each kind covers, and how to find a doctor or dentist who takes it.

Everywhere

Most rich countries cover everyone through the government. The United States mixes government plans, job plans and plans you buy. If you're in another country, ask what its public health system covers; often it's more than you'd guess.

Medicaid: for low income

Run by each state with federal money. In Ohio it covers adults with low income, children, pregnant people, and people with disabilities.

  • Covers: doctors, hospitals, prescriptions, mental health and addiction care, eye care, and rides to appointments in many cases.
  • Dental for adults in Ohio: checkups and cleanings, fillings and pulling teeth. Dentures need approval first. Your plan's handbook lists the limits.
  • Find a dentist who takes it: call the member number on your Medicaid card and ask for three dentists taking new patients. Or try a community health center (opens in a new tab).
  • Apply or renew: Ohio Benefits (opens in a new tab) or the Ohio Medicaid hotline, 800-324-8680. Open every letter about renewal and answer by the date on it.

Medicare: 65 and older

Federal coverage for people 65 and older and some younger people with disabilities.

  • Part A is hospital care. Part B is doctors and outpatient care. Part D is prescriptions.
  • Original Medicare doesn't cover most dental care, eyeglasses or hearing aids. Many Medicare Advantage plans add some of these.
  • Change plans each year from October 15 to December 7.
  • Free, unbiased help in Ohio: OSHIIP, 800-686-1578. Compare doctors and plans at Medicare Care Compare (opens in a new tab).

The Marketplace: buy your own plan

  • Sign up at HealthCare.gov (opens in a new tab) from November 1 to January 15. Losing coverage, moving or having a baby lets you sign up within 60 days.
  • Help paying depends on your income. The site shows your price before you pick.
  • Preventive care like checkups, many screenings and vaccines costs nothing extra on these plans.

VA health care

  • Apply at VA.gov (opens in a new tab) or call 877-222-8387.
  • VA dental is limited to some veterans, such as those rated 100% service-connected or with a service-connected dental problem.
  • If you're enrolled in VA health care, you can buy low-cost VA dental insurance (opens in a new tab).
  • Find a VA clinic: VA locations (opens in a new tab).

No coverage? You can still get care

  • Community health centers (opens in a new tab) charge on a sliding scale by income, including dental at many of them.
  • Emergency rooms must check and stabilize you even if you can't pay. See your rights as a patient.
  • Hospitals run by nonprofits must have a financial assistance policy. Ask for it before you pay a big bill.
