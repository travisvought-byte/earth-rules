Renting vs buying.

Buying a home can build wealth, but it costs more up front and ties you down. Renting keeps you free to move.

HomeTopic 4 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: This compares the real costs of renting and owning, and points to free help if you want to buy.

Why it matters: A home is the biggest purchase most people make, and the wrong timing can cost thousands.

Do this first

  1. Ask yourself if you might move in the next few years. If so, renting is often safer.
  2. Save an emergency fund first, usually 3 to 6 months of expenses.
  3. Plan for a down payment and closing costs of 2% to 5% of the price.
  4. Add taxes, insurance and repairs to the monthly mortgage.
  5. Talk to a free HUD-approved housing counselor at 800-569-4287.

Numbers to call

  • 800-569-4287HUD housing counselor
  • 888-362-6432Ohio Housing Finance Agency

What buying costs up front

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) says:

  • Down payment: some loans need as little as 3% to 3.5% of the price. If you put down less than 20%, you will likely pay mortgage insurance each month.
  • Closing costs: plan on 2% to 5% of the price, on top of the down payment.
  • Emergency fund: keep savings for emergencies, usually 3 to 6 months of expenses, before you spend on a down payment.

What owning costs every month

The mortgage is only part of it. Owners also pay property taxes, homeowners insurance, water and utilities, and these costs tend to rise over the years. Owners pay for all maintenance and repairs too, so keep an emergency fund. Renters have more flexibility to move.

How long you will stay

Buying and selling both cost money in fees, commissions and taxes. The CFPB says you want to stay long enough to make those costs worth it. Buying can be risky if there is a chance you will move within the next few years, or if your income may not stay steady.

Free help

HUD-approved housing counselors help with buying, renting and avoiding foreclosure. Call 800-569-4287 to find one. Foreclosure and eviction counseling is always free. Other classes may have a small fee, which must be waived if you cannot pay. Renting? Know your rights at tenants' rights. See also credit and money.

Everywhere

Everywhere, buying costs more up front and renting gives more freedom. The longer you stay in one place, the more owning tends to make sense.

In the United States

In the US, the CFPB has free homebuying guides (opens in a new tab). FHA loans can need as little as 3.5% down, and some conventional loans as little as 3%.

In Ohio

The Ohio Housing Finance Agency offers down payment help for low- and moderate-income buyers: 3% of the price on a conventional loan, or 3.5% on an FHA, VA or USDA loan (since July 2025). It is forgiven after seven years, but you pay it back if you sell sooner. You apply through an approved lender. Learn more at myohiohome.org (opens in a new tab) or call 888-362-6432.
