Breakups and divorce.

A breakup can turn your whole life upside down, but there are clear steps and free help for the hard parts.

Love and familyTopic 8 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: How to get through the first weeks of a breakup, and the basics of divorce, custody and child support.

Why it matters: Heartbreak makes it hard to think, and that's exactly when choices about money, kids and safety matter most.

Do this first

  1. Lean on friends and keep up sleep, food and routine for the first few weeks.
  2. Don't send messages late at night. Write it down and wait until morning.
  3. If your ex is controlling or scary, make a safety plan with an advocate.
  4. Keep kids out of the middle. Never use them to pass messages.
  5. Get free legal information before you sign anything.

Numbers to call

  • 1-800-799-7233National Domestic Violence Hotline
  • 988Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

The first few weeks

  • Feeling sad, angry, numb or relieved is normal. Feelings come in waves.
  • Tell a few people you trust what happened so you aren't alone with it.
  • Pause before you text. Messages sent at 2 a.m. are the ones people regret.
  • Mute or unfollow if seeing their posts keeps the wound open.
  • If you feel hopeless or think about hurting yourself, call or text 988.

If your ex is controlling

Abuse can get worse after someone leaves. If your ex threatens, follows or controls you, talk to an advocate at the National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. In danger right now, call 911.

Divorce, custody and support

  • Custody decisions are based on the best interests of the child. Courts look at things like each parent's ability to care for the child, the child's ties to siblings and family, and sometimes the child's own wishes. The details depend on your state.
  • Child support goes through your state or local child support office. They can help set up an order, collect payments, and change the order if your income or family changes.
  • Keep kids out of adult fights. Let them love both parents if it's safe.

Free legal help

LawHelp.org (opens in a new tab) connects you to free legal aid groups in every state, with forms and guides on your rights. See also money for untangling shared bills and moving if you're changing homes.

Everywhere

Most places decide custody by asking what is best for the child. Breakups are painful everywhere, and support from friends and routine helps people heal.

In the United States

Divorce and custody are handled by state courts, so rules differ by state. To sign up for child support or change an order, find your state's office through the federal Office of Child Support Services (opens in a new tab).

In Ohio

Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab) has free how-to guides and court forms for divorce, custody and child support, plus a Find a Lawyer tool and a free account to save and fill out forms.
