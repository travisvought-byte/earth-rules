Voting.

Your vote is your say in who runs things and what the rules are.

CivicTopic 2 of 6

In 10 seconds

What it is: Voting is how citizens choose leaders and decide ballot issues, in person, early, or by mail.

Why it matters: Elected people decide taxes, schools, police and roads, and a few votes often decide local races.

Do this first

  1. Register to vote at vote.gov, or update your registration if you moved or changed your name.
  2. Check that you are registered and find your polling place.
  3. Look at a sample ballot before you go so nothing surprises you.
  4. Learn your state's ID rule and bring the right ID.
  5. Pick how you will vote: early, by mail, or on Election Day.

Numbers to call

  • 1-877-767-6446Ohio Secretary of State

Get registered

Start at vote.gov (opens in a new tab). It shows how to register in your state, your deadline, and how to check or update your registration after you move or change your name. Some states let you register on Election Day. Others close registration weeks before.

Know where and what

  • Polling place. Your polling place is set by your home address. It can change between elections, so check each time. You can find it by checking your voter registration or asking your local election office.
  • Sample ballot. It lists the candidates and the issues you will vote on. Read it at home and make notes.
  • Help at the polls. If you need a disability accommodation, tell your election office ahead of time.

Read more about your rights at the polls on voter rights.

Everywhere

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights says the will of the people is the basis of government, shown through regular, genuine elections with equal voting and a secret ballot. Rules on who can vote and how differ by country, so check your national election office.

In the United States

Each state runs its own elections and sets its own rules for voter ID, deadlines and mail voting. Most states ask for ID to vote in person. Use USA.gov (opens in a new tab) or vote.gov (opens in a new tab) to find your state's rules. Military members and citizens living abroad can use the Federal Voting Assistance Program.

In Ohio

  • Register at least 30 days before Election Day. You can register online, by mail, or in person at your county board of elections or a BMV office.
  • Bring photo ID to vote in person. It must show your name and photo and not be expired. Accepted: an Ohio driver's license, Ohio state ID card, or BMV interim ID form, a US passport or passport card, or a US military, Ohio National Guard, or VA ID card.
  • Vote early in person. Early voting starts the day after registration closes and ends at 5 p.m. on the Sunday before Election Day, at your county board of elections or the place it picks.
  • Vote by mail. Any registered Ohio voter can vote absentee. No reason needed. Your request must include ID, like your Ohio driver's license number or the last four digits of your Social Security number. Your county board must get your request by close of business seven days before Election Day. Your ballot must reach the board by the time polls close on Election Day.

Call the Ohio Secretary of State at 1-877-767-6446 or your county board of elections with questions.
