EARTH RULES · LOVE AND FAMILY

Pregnancy and birth.

Getting care early and knowing the warning signs are the two biggest things you can do for a healthy pregnancy.

Love and familyTopic 5 of 9

In 10 seconds

What it is: Pregnancy is the time, about nine months, when a baby grows inside the body before birth.

Why it matters: Many problems during and after pregnancy can be prevented or treated when they are caught early.

Do this first

  1. Take a home test, then call a doctor or clinic as soon as you think you are pregnant.
  2. Take 400 mcg of folic acid every day, starting before pregnancy if you can.
  3. Stop alcohol, smoking and vaping. It is never too late to quit.
  4. Go to every prenatal visit.
  5. Learn the urgent warning signs, which last up to a year after birth.
  6. Apply for Medicaid and WIC if money is tight.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency
  • 1-833-852-6262National Maternal Mental Health Hotline (call or text, 24/7)
  • 988Suicide and Crisis Lifeline
  • 1-800-784-8669Quit smoking help (1-800-QUIT-NOW)

Early signs and prenatal care

A missed period is the most common sign, but many people notice others first: sore breasts, tiredness, nausea, needing to pee often, headaches, mood swings or light spotting. Some people have no signs at all. These can have other causes, so see a health care provider to confirm.

Call for your first prenatal visit as soon as you think you are pregnant. It is often around week 8. For a healthy pregnancy, visits are usually:

  • About once a month until week 28
  • About every two weeks from week 28 to 36
  • About once a week from week 36 until birth

What to avoid

  • Alcohol. There is no known safe amount during pregnancy or while trying to get pregnant. If you drank before you knew, stop now and tell your provider.
  • Smoking and vaping. These raise the risk of early birth, low birth weight and sudden infant death. Free help: 1-800-QUIT-NOW.
  • Marijuana. The CDC discourages it during pregnancy.
  • Opioids. Do not start or stop prescription opioids or treatment medicines without talking to your provider.

Urgent warning signs: get care right away

The CDC says to get medical care immediately for any of these, during pregnancy or up to a year after:

  • A headache that will not go away or gets worse, or vision changes
  • Fainting or dizziness
  • Fever of 100.4°F or higher
  • Very swollen hands or face
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or a pounding heartbeat
  • Severe belly pain, or severe vomiting
  • Baby moving less, or bleeding or fluid leaking during pregnancy
  • Heavy bleeding after birth, like soaking a pad in an hour
  • A swollen, red, painful leg or arm
  • Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby
  • Extreme tiredness, or not being able to care for your baby

Tell any doctor or nurse that you are pregnant or were pregnant in the last year.

After birth: your mood matters

The "baby blues," feeling sad, worried and tired for a few days after birth, are common and pass. Postpartum depression is stronger and lasts longer. About 1 in 8 new mothers report signs of it. It can mean crying a lot, anger, feeling distant from your baby, or doubting you can care for them. It is treatable, and most people get better with treatment. Talk to your provider, or call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262, any time, free. See parenting and mental health first aid.

Everywhere

Wherever you live, start care early, take folic acid, avoid alcohol and tobacco, and take warning signs seriously. If something feels wrong, say so, and ask the people around you to listen.

In the United States

Medicaid covers many pregnant people, and WIC gives healthy food and nutrition help to people who are pregnant, up to 6 months after pregnancy, breastfeeding up to the baby's first birthday, and to children up to age 5. If you already get Medicaid or SNAP, you may already meet WIC's income rule. See coverage and basics.

In Ohio

Ohio Medicaid covers you during pregnancy and for 12 months after the pregnancy ends. This started April 1, 2022. Apply online at Ohio Benefits, call 1-844-640-6446, or go to your county Job and Family Services office.
