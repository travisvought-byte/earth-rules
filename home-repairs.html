Basic home repairs.

A few small skills save money, and a few safety checks save lives.

Skills and funTopic 2 of 4

In 10 seconds

What it is: These are simple home fixes and checks most people can do, and the signs it is time to call a pro.

Why it matters: Knowing where things are and what to check can stop a small problem from becoming a flood, a fire or worse.

Do this first

  1. Find your main water shutoff and breaker panel now, before you need them.
  2. Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms once a month.
  3. Test GFCI outlets once a month with the test and reset buttons.
  4. If you smell gas, leave right away and call from outside.
  5. If a CO alarm sounds, get outside, then call 911.

Numbers to call

  • 911Emergency

Safety checks

  • Smoke alarms: test them every month. Put one on every level, and inside and outside each bedroom. Replace the whole alarm every 10 years.
  • Carbon monoxide (CO) alarms: put one on every level and outside sleeping areas. Test them every month. If one sounds, get outside right away, then call 911.
  • Gas smell: leave right away. Do not use light switches, electronics or your phone until you are out. Call the gas company from outside.

Easy fixes

  • GFCI outlets (the ones with buttons, near water): plug in a lamp and press TEST. The light should go off. Press RESET and it should come back. If not, have an electrician check it.
  • Running toilet: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 10 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leak, often a worn flapper. The EPA says to replace flappers at least every five years.
  • Tripped breaker: flip it fully off, then back on. If it keeps tripping, call an electrician.
  • Slow drain: try a plunger or a cheap drain snake first.

A basic toolkit

A hammer, a screwdriver with changeable tips, pliers, an adjustable wrench, a tape measure, a utility knife, a plunger, a flashlight, duct tape and a level will handle most small jobs.

Help paying for repairs

  • USDA Section 504 helps very-low-income owners in rural areas. Loans up to $40,000 at 1% interest. Grants up to $10,000 for owners 62 and older. Apply year-round at your local USDA Rural Development office.
  • Your local Area Agency on Aging and city or county housing office may know of other repair programs. See aging and caregiving.

Everywhere

Everywhere, working smoke alarms and knowing how to shut off water and power make a home safer. Gas and big electrical work belong to licensed pros.

In the United States

In the US, the US Fire Administration and the Consumer Product Safety Commission publish free home safety guides. Renters: repairs may be your landlord's job. See tenants' rights.

In Ohio

Ohio's 12 Area Agencies on Aging offer home maintenance, repairs and changes for older adults in some areas. Call 1-866-243-5678 to reach yours.
