Critical thinking.

Critical thinking just means checking before you believe, share or buy.

BrainTopic 2 of 7

In 10 seconds

What it is: Critical thinking is a set of questions you ask to figure out whether a claim is true.

Why it matters: Ads, posts, headlines and people all want you to believe things, and some of them are wrong.

Do this first

  1. Ask who is behind this information.
  2. Ask what the evidence is.
  3. Open a new tab and see what other sources say.
  4. Find the original source, not someone's summary of it.
  5. Ask yourself what would change your mind.

Read sideways

Researchers watched professional fact checkers, history professors and college students judge websites. The fact checkers did best. They asked three questions:

  1. Who's behind the information?
  2. What's the evidence?
  3. What do other sources say?

Fact checkers don't stay on a page to judge it. They leave it. This is called lateral reading: keep the page open, open a new tab, search for the author or group, and compare. A nice-looking page proves nothing.

Together is not the same as because

When two things go up and down together, that's correlation. It doesn't mean one causes the other. Ice cream and sunscreen sales rise together, but neither causes the other. Hot weather drives both.

Causation means one thing really brings about the other, like smoking raising the risk of lung cancer. Proving it usually takes careful, controlled studies.

More good questions

  • How common is it to begin with? A scary story about something rare can make it feel common. Ask for the real numbers.
  • Who gains if I believe this? Money, votes and clicks are strong reasons to bend the truth.
  • What would change my mind? If the answer is "nothing," that's a feeling, not a conclusion.
  • Am I upset? Strong emotion is a signal to slow down. See manipulation.

Everywhere

These questions work in any language and any country, on a news site, a sales pitch, a rumor or your own first guess. You don't have to be an expert. You just have to check.

In the United States
