EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Your rights as a student.

School is where you learn your rights. These are yours while you're there.

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to a free public education, whatever your immigration status. Source: Plyler v. Doe (1982) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to free speech at school. Schools can limit speech that substantially disrupts class, but they can't silence a view just because they dislike it.Source: Tinker v. Des Moines (1969) (opens in a new tab)
  • You, or your parents if you're under 18 and in K-12, have the right to see school records within 45 days and ask for corrections. Source: 34 CFR § 99.10 (opens in a new tab); 20 U.S. Code § 1232g (opens in a new tab)
  • If you have a disability, you have the right to a free appropriate public education, with an IEP or a 504 plan. Source: 20 U.S. Code § 1412 (opens in a new tab); 29 U.S. Code § 794 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to an education free of sex discrimination and sexual harassment. Source: 20 U.S. Code § 1681 (opens in a new tab)
  • If you don't have a stable home, you have the right to enroll right away, without the usual papers. You can stay at your school of origin when that's in your best interest.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 11432 (opens in a new tab)

Ohio-specific student rights are on the way. Complaints about discrimination at school go to the US Department of Education (opens in a new tab)'s Office for Civil Rights.

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
