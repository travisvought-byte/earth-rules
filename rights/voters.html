EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Your rights as a voter.

Your vote is your voice in what the government does next.

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to vote at 18, whatever your race, color or sex. No poll tax can be charged in federal elections.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 15 (opens in a new tab); U.S. Constitution, Amendment 19 (opens in a new tab); U.S. Constitution, Amendment 24 (opens in a new tab); U.S. Constitution, Amendment 26 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to bring someone to help you vote if you need it because of a disability or trouble reading. Anyone you choose can help, except your employer or union officer.Source: 52 U.S. Code § 10508 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a provisional ballot if your name isn't on the list. It's counted once your eligibility is confirmed.Source: 52 U.S. Code § 21082 (opens in a new tab)
  • In some counties, you have the right to voting materials in your language. Source: 52 U.S. Code § 10503 (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have the right to vote if you're in line when polls close. Polls are open 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. Stay in line.Source: Ohio Revised Code 3501.32 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to time off work to vote. Your employer can't fire or threaten you for taking a reasonable amount of time.Source: Ohio Revised Code 3599.06 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to vote with any of the accepted photo IDs. Without one, you can cast a provisional ballot and show ID within four days.Source: Ohio Revised Code 3505.18 (opens in a new tab)
  • Your right to vote returns when you leave prison. A felony record doesn't stop you from voting in Ohio once you're released. Re-register.Source: Ohio Revised Code 2961.01 (opens in a new tab)

Turned away or intimidated? Ask for a provisional ballot, then report it to the Ohio Secretary of State (opens in a new tab) or the Justice Department (opens in a new tab).

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
