EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Your rights as a patient.

Your body, your records, your decisions.

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to see and get a copy of your medical records. Your provider or health plan must act within 30 days, with one 30-day extension if they tell you why in writing. Fees are limited to a reasonable cost.Source: 45 CFR § 164.524 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to ask for a correction to your records. If they say no, you can add your own statement of disagreement.Source: 45 CFR § 164.526 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to emergency care whether or not you can pay. Any hospital with an emergency department that takes Medicare must screen you and stabilize an emergency, or transfer you safely, before asking about money.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 1395dd (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to be protected from most surprise medical bills. For emergency care, and for out-of-network doctors at an in-network hospital, you pay only your in-network share. Uninsured or paying yourself? You have the right to a good faith estimate before care.Source: CMS: No Surprises Act (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a free interpreter. Health programs that receive federal money must provide language help and sign language interpreters at no cost to you.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 18116 (ACA Section 1557) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to make your own care decisions in advance. Hospitals and nursing homes in Medicare must tell you about your right to write an advance directive.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 1395cc(f) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to privacy for your health information. Most providers can't share it without your permission, outside of treatment, billing and a few other uses.Source: 45 CFR § 164.502 (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have the right to inspect your records free, or get a copy, within a reasonable time. Ohio caps what providers can charge for copies.Source: Ohio Revised Code 3701.74 (opens in a new tab); Ohio Revised Code 3701.741 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to name someone to make health decisions if you can't. That's a health care power of attorney.Source: Ohio Revised Code 1337.12 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to decide in writing about life support at the end of life. That's a living will.Source: Ohio Revised Code 2133.02 (opens in a new tab)
  • If you live in a nursing home, you have a written bill of rights. It covers dignity, privacy, visitors, your own money and belongings, and taking part in your care plan.Source: Ohio Revised Code 3721.13 (opens in a new tab)
  • If you're in mental health treatment, you have specific rights. These include humane care, privacy, contact with family and friends, and talking with a lawyer.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5122.29 (opens in a new tab)

Problem with records or privacy? File with HHS Office for Civil Rights (opens in a new tab) within 180 days.

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
