EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Your rights at work.

You trade your time for pay. The terms have a floor, and these rights are it.

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to at least the minimum wage, and overtime pay. The federal minimum is $7.25 an hour. Most hourly workers earn time and a half after 40 hours in a week.Source: 29 U.S. Code § 206 (opens in a new tab); 29 U.S. Code § 207 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to work free of discrimination. That covers race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation and gender identity), national origin, age 40 and over, disability and genetic information. Most of these laws apply at employers with 15 or more workers.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 2000e-2 (opens in a new tab); Bostock v. Clayton County (2020) (opens in a new tab); 29 U.S. Code § 623 (opens in a new tab); 42 U.S. Code § 12112 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to equal pay for equal work, regardless of sex. Source: 29 U.S. Code § 206(d) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to job-protected leave for a new child or a serious health condition. Up to 12 weeks unpaid a year, if your employer has 50 or more employees nearby and you've worked there a year and 1,250 hours.Source: 29 U.S. Code § 2612 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to reasonable accommodations for pregnancy, and break time and a private place to pump. Source: 42 U.S. Code § 2000gg-1 (opens in a new tab); 29 U.S. Code § 218d (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to talk with coworkers about pay and working conditions, and to organize. This protects most private-sector workers, union or not, but not supervisors.Source: 29 U.S. Code § 157 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a safe workplace and to report hazards. Your employer can't punish you for a safety complaint. A retaliation complaint must reach OSHA within 30 days.Source: 29 U.S. Code § 660(c) (opens in a new tab); OSHA: file a complaint (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to your job back after military service. Source: 38 U.S. Code § 4312 (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have the right to Ohio's higher minimum wage: $11.00 an hour in 2026. It applies at employers with more than $405,000 in yearly gross receipts. Tipped workers get at least $5.50 plus tips that reach the full minimum. It rises with inflation each January, to $11.40 on January 1, 2027.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article II, Section 34a (opens in a new tab); Ohio Department of Commerce: minimum wage (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to work free of discrimination at Ohio employers with four or more workers. Ohio adds military status and ancestry to the federal list.Source: Ohio Revised Code 4112.02 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to take a reasonable amount of time to vote without being fired or threatened. Source: Ohio Revised Code 3599.06 (opens in a new tab)

Unpaid wages: Wage and Hour Division (opens in a new tab), free and confidential. Discrimination: EEOC (opens in a new tab) within 300 days in Ohio, or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission (opens in a new tab). Deadlines are short, so act soon.

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
