EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Stopped, arrested or accused.

You have rights at every step: on the street, in a car, in custody and in court.

What to say

  • "I'm going to remain silent."
  • "I want a lawyer."
  • "I don't consent to a search."
  • "Am I free to go?"

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to remain silent. You don't have to answer questions about where you've been, where you're going or what you did. To use it, say so out loud; silence alone may not count.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 5 (opens in a new tab); Berghuis v. Thompkins (2010) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a lawyer, and to a free one if you can't afford it. Once you ask for a lawyer, questioning is supposed to stop until one is there. If you're charged with a crime that can mean jail, the court appoints a lawyer at no cost.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 6 (opens in a new tab); Miranda v. Arizona (1966) (opens in a new tab); Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to refuse consent to a search. Police may still search if they have a warrant or another legal reason, but your "no" matters later in court. Don't physically resist; say it.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 4 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to ask whether you are free to go. If you're not under arrest or being detained, you can calmly walk away. A traffic stop can last only as long as its purpose needs.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 4 (opens in a new tab); Rodriguez v. United States (2015) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to know the charges against you. You also have the right to a speedy and public trial, a jury, to see and question the witnesses against you, and to call your own.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 6 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to bail that isn't excessive, and to be free from cruel and unusual punishment. Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 8 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right not to be tried twice for the same crime. Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 5 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to tell your consulate if you're not a US citizen. If you're arrested, ask the police to notify your country's consulate.Source: State Department: consular notification (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have the right to stay silent beyond your name, address and date of birth. In a public place, if an officer reasonably suspects you of a crime or of witnessing a serious one, Ohio requires you to give those three things. Nothing more is required. Drivers must also show a license when asked.Source: Ohio Revised Code 2921.29 (opens in a new tab); Ohio Revised Code 4507.35 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a lawyer, a speedy public trial by an impartial jury, and to meet the witnesses against you face to face. You can't be forced to testify against yourself.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 10 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to a trial within set time limits. For a felony, trial must start within 270 days of arrest. Each day you're held in jail on that charge alone counts as three.Source: Ohio Revised Code 2945.71 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to bail in most cases. Ohio judges set bail and its conditions; excessive bail is barred.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 9 (opens in a new tab)
  • You keep your right to vote if you're charged but not convicted, and it comes back when you leave prison. People held in jail awaiting trial, or for a misdemeanor, can vote absentee. After release from a felony sentence, re-register.Source: Ohio Revised Code 2961.01 (opens in a new tab)

If you are the victim

  • If a crime is committed against you in Ohio, you have the right to be treated with fairness and respect for your safety, dignity and privacy. You also have the right to notice of public court dates, to be present, to be heard at release, plea, sentencing and parole hearings, to reasonable protection from the accused, to restitution, and to written notice of these rights.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 10a (Marsy's Law) (opens in a new tab)
  • In a federal case, you have similar rights. These include being protected from the accused, notified of hearings, heard, and paid restitution.Source: 18 U.S. Code § 3771 (opens in a new tab)

Keep your hands visible, stay calm, and don't run or physically resist, even if you believe the stop is wrong. Challenge it later with a lawyer. Write down badge numbers and car numbers when you can.

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
