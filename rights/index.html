EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Your rights.

What's yours, wherever you are, and the law that says so.

Your rights are already yours

Nobody hands you your rights. The Declaration of Independence calls them "unalienable." The Ninth Amendment says the rights written down are not the only ones you keep. Ohio's Constitution opens with the line that every person has "certain inalienable rights."

Laws don't create these rights. They write them down and set limits on what governments, landlords, employers and businesses can do to you. Each page below says what's yours, in plain words, and links to the law that backs it up.

By place: where you are changes what's covered

  • Everywhere in the US: the Constitution and federal law. These are the floor; no state can go below them.
  • Your state: its own constitution and laws can add more. Every situation page lists Ohio's, and more states are coming.
  • Your city: many cities add protections, such as for sexual orientation in housing or for paid sick time. Check your city's code.
  • At the border and airport security: agents can search bags and devices with less cause than police need inside the country.
  • In another country: its laws apply to you. As a US citizen arrested abroad, you have the right to ask that the US embassy be told. See the State Department's guide (opens in a new tab).

How to use a right

  • Name it out loud: "I'm using my right to remain silent." Some rights only count once you say them.
  • Put it in writing, keep copies, and note dates and names.
  • Watch the deadline. Many complaints must be filed within 30 days to a year.
  • Get help free: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab) in Ohio, or dial 211 and ask for legal aid anywhere.

This is general information about the law, checked against the sources on each page. It isn't legal advice for your situation. For that, talk with a lawyer or legal aid.
