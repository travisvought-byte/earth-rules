Your rights as a renter.

A lease is a deal between two sides. These are yours no matter what it says.

HomeTopic 6 of 6

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to rent or buy a home without discrimination. Landlords and sellers can't turn you away, charge you more or treat you differently because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status (kids under 18) or disability.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 3604 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to reasonable accommodations for a disability. That includes an assistance animal in a no-pets building, or a reserved parking space, when you need it.Source: 42 U.S. Code § 3604(f) (opens in a new tab)
  • If you're on active duty, you have the right to end a lease early for orders. That covers a permanent change of station or a deployment of 90 days or more.Source: 50 U.S. Code § 3955 (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have the right to a safe place that's kept up. Your landlord must make repairs, keep the place fit to live in, and keep electrical, plumbing, heating and sanitation working.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.04 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to notice before your landlord comes in. Twenty-four hours is presumed reasonable, except in an emergency.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to pay rent to the court instead of the landlord when repairs aren't made. Give written notice. If it isn't fixed within a reasonable time, or 30 days at most, and your rent is current, you can deposit rent with the clerk of court. Some small landlords are exempt.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.07 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to your security deposit back, or a written list of deductions, within 30 days. Give your landlord a forwarding address in writing. If they wrongfully keep money, you can recover it plus an equal amount in damages and attorney fees.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.16 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to complain without retaliation. Your landlord can't raise rent, cut services or evict you for reporting code violations or asking for repairs.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.02 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right not to be locked out or have utilities shut off to force you out. Only a court can order an eviction.Source: Ohio Revised Code 5321.15 (opens in a new tab); Ohio Revised Code 1923.04 (opens in a new tab)
  • Ohio adds ancestry and military status to the list of protected classes in housing. Source: Ohio Revised Code 4112.02 (opens in a new tab)

Housing discrimination: report to HUD (opens in a new tab) within one year, or to the Ohio Civil Rights Commission (opens in a new tab). If you get an eviction notice, go to every court date: that's where you have the right to be heard.

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
