EARTH RULES · YOUR RIGHTS

Rights every person has.

These are yours because you're a person. They limit what any government can do to you.

Everywhere in the United States

  • You have the right to speak, worship or not, gather peacefully and petition the government. The government can't punish you for your views or your religion. Private employers and websites are not the government, so their own rules can still apply.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 1 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to be secure in your person, home, papers and belongings. Government searches and seizures must be reasonable, and most need a warrant signed by a judge.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 4 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to due process. No government can take your life, liberty or property without fair procedures: notice of what's happening and a real chance to be heard.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 5 (opens in a new tab); U.S. Constitution, Amendment 14 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to equal protection of the laws. Every person, citizen or not, is covered. The Fourteenth Amendment says "any person," and the Supreme Court has read it that way since 1886.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 14 (opens in a new tab); Yick Wo v. Hopkins (1886) (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to keep and bear arms. States and cities set rules on carrying, buying and where weapons can go, so check the law where you are.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 2 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to be free from slavery and forced labor. The only exception the Constitution writes in is punishment for a crime after conviction.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 13 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have rights that aren't written down in a list. The Ninth Amendment says naming some rights does not deny or shrink the others the people keep.Source: U.S. Constitution, Amendment 9 (opens in a new tab)

In Ohio, you also have these

  • You have inalienable rights, and Ohio's Constitution says so first. Article I, Section 1 begins: "All men are, by nature, free and independent, and have certain inalienable rights," including enjoying and defending life and liberty, owning property and seeking happiness and safety.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 1 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to speak, write and publish your views on any subject. You are responsible for abuse of that right, such as defamation.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 11 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right to be secure in your person, house, papers and possessions. Ohio's own search and seizure protection sits alongside the federal one.Source: Ohio Constitution, Article I, Section 14 (opens in a new tab)
  • You have the right not to be treated differently because of race, color, religion, sex, military status, national origin, disability, age or ancestry. This covers jobs, housing and businesses open to the public in Ohio.Source: Ohio Revised Code 4112.02 (opens in a new tab)

A right works best when you name it. Say it out loud, put it in writing, keep copies, and write down dates and names. Free legal help in Ohio: Ohio Legal Help (opens in a new tab). Anywhere else, dial 211 and ask for legal aid.
