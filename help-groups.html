Addictions you can't afford

An addiction is anything you keep doing after it starts costing you money, health, work or people. Alcohol, drugs, gambling, spending and nicotine are the common ones. It isn't a moral failing, and you don't have to hit bottom to get help.

Groups that meet everywhere, free

  • Alcohol: Alcoholics Anonymous (opens in a new tab) meets in most towns worldwide and online.
  • Drugs: Narcotics Anonymous (opens in a new tab), any drug, including alcohol.
  • A science-based option: SMART Recovery (opens in a new tab), for any addiction, with no higher power required.
  • Gambling: Gamblers Anonymous (opens in a new tab).
  • Spending and debt: Debtors Anonymous (opens in a new tab).
  • Family and friends of someone drinking: Al-Anon (opens in a new tab) and Alateen.

Meetings are free. You can sit in the back and just listen. Try a few different groups; each one feels different.

Everywhere

  • Treatment is real medicine. For opioids and alcohol, there are medicines that cut the risk of relapse and death. Ask a doctor.
  • Stopping heavy drinking or some drugs suddenly can be dangerous. Get medical help to stop safely.

In the United States

  • FindTreatment.gov (opens in a new tab): every licensed treatment place, searchable by insurance and cost.
  • SAMHSA's helpline: 1-800-662-4357, free, confidential, 24/7, in English and Spanish.
  • Quitline for tobacco and vaping: 1-800-QUIT-NOW.

In Ohio

  • Ohio's Problem Gambling Helpline: 1-800-589-9966, 24/7.
  • Ohio Medicaid covers addiction treatment. See health coverage.
  • Dial 211 for meetings and treatment near you.
